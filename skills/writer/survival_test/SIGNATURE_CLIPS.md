# Clip #01: Roza Vidal — "The Velocity Architect"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: Two timelines collide at speed — characters outrun their feelings until the crash.

## [SETTING]
Interior hallway of a fourth-floor walk-up at 11:47 PM. Narrow corridor, flickering fluorescent above a buzzing ballast, three take-out menus taped to a fire door, the smell of fish sauce from one apartment and bleach from another. A toddler is crying two doors down. Someone's TV is running a weather report in Tagalog. The hallway runs long enough that the vanishing point disappears into stacked cardboard boxes.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
Handheld 16mm blown up to digital, grain visible, sodium-vapor color correction, shallow depth of field that can't quite keep up with the action. Natural sound, no score, dialogue overlapping like a Cassavetes fight. Camera chases, never leads. Lens breathing. Boom mic just barely out of frame. Low-budget urban realism with screwball choreography.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Dasha**: 29, stripper coming off a double, one press-on nail missing, hauling a rolling suitcase with a busted wheel, mascara from three hours ago, her brother's phone to her ear, her own phone vibrating in her bra. **Voice Style**: Russian-inflected Brooklyn, smoke-rasped, overlaps everyone, fast.

**Ma**: 58, housecoat over jeans, reading glasses on her nose, a ladle in one hand, blocking the apartment door. **Voice Style**: Russian, maternal, interrogative, fast-paced.

**Edik (V.O., phone)**: Dasha's cousin, panicked. **Voice Style**: Male, wheezy, hyperventilating, Russian-American.

## [SHOT LIST]

**[SHOT 1: MS - EYE LEVEL, HANDHELD TRACKING]**

Dasha half-runs down the hallway dragging the suitcase, one wheel scraping; Dasha pins a phone to one ear with a shoulder; Dasha's other phone buzzes against Dasha's ribs;

DASHA (Breathless, Overlapping): "Edik I'm literally in the hallway, I'm in the hallway right now—";

EDIK (V.O., Panicked): "—she cannot know, Dasha, she cannot—";

SFX: **SUITCASE WHEEL GRIND; FLUORESCENT BUZZ; MUFFLED TV WEATHER REPORT**

**[SHOT 2: MCU - EYE LEVEL, HANDHELD WHIP PAN]**

Ma's door opens mid-stride; Ma fills the frame holding a dripping ladle; Dasha does not stop, does not slow, simply rotates around Ma like a planet;

MA (Suspicious, Loud): "Where have you been, whose suitcase is that—";

DASHA (Not Looking): "Sasha's, it's Sasha's, the borscht is burning—";

SFX: **LADLE DRIP ON LINOLEUM**

**[SHOT 3: MS - LOW ANGLE, HANDHELD]**

Dasha shoves past Ma into the apartment; Dasha's second phone finally answered with a chin-press against the suitcase handle; a man's shoe, unfamiliar, sits in the entryway; Dasha clocks the shoe and keeps walking;

DASHA (Flat, Not Slowing): "Ma. Whose shoe.";

MA (Off-Screen, Defensive): "Don't change the subject—";

SFX: **APARTMENT DOOR SLAM; DISTANT TODDLER CRY; PHONE BUZZ**

**Tone**: Screwball catastrophe at full sprint — three crises, three languages, one hallway, and the feeling arrives after the door closes.

# Clip #02: Meret Still — "The Body Architect"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: The body becomes something the character didn't authorize — not damage, mutiny.

## [SETTING]
Interior of a public-pool changing room at 6:14 AM, off-season. Wet tile, sixty years old, grouted with something darker than grout. A long bench. A mirror that runs the entire wall, lit from above by a caged incandescent bulb that makes every shadow unflattering. Room temperature 58°F. The air smells like chlorine and copper. A single shower drips at an irregular interval. Two elderly women are audibly toweling off behind a plastic partition, unseen, their voices carrying.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
Zone-focused medium-format digital, natural light only, no color grade — raw skin tones, visible capillaries, every pore legible. Static tripod or slow dolly, never handheld. Sound mixed close to the body: breath, wet foot on tile, the small click of a jaw. No score. No music. Practical effects entirely — prosthetic work, not VFX. Flesh-as-document realism. Unflinching. The camera does not flatter.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Ines**: 51, swimmer's shoulders, a surgical scar across the sternum, wet hair dripping, wrapped in a thin white towel that is too small. A small raised ridge has appeared along the left collarbone in the last six days. **Voice Style**: None — does not speak. Breath audible.

**Woman (Off-Screen)**: elderly, Polish-accented English, mid-conversation with unseen companion. **Voice Style**: Matter-of-fact, not addressing Ines.

## [SHOT LIST]

**[SHOT 1: ECU - EYE LEVEL, STATIC]**

A single bead of pool water travels down Ines' collarbone and stops; the bead does not continue; the bead is held by a small, smooth, subcutaneous ridge that was not there last week; the ridge is the temperature of the room, colder than the surrounding skin;

SFX: **DRIP; DISTANT TOWELING; FAINT HUM OF BALLAST**

**[SHOT 2: MCU - EYE LEVEL, SLOW DOLLY IN]**

Ines is reflected in the wall mirror; Ines does not move; Ines' fingertips find the ridge and press; Ines' breath catches once and holds; the ridge does not yield; Ines' expression does not change but Ines' jaw sets;

WOMAN (Off-Screen, Conversational): "—and I told her, the body does what the body wants, we only live in it—";

SFX: **HELD BREATH; FINGERPAD ON SKIN**

**[SHOT 3: CU - HIGH ANGLE, STATIC]**

Ines' hand lowers from the collarbone; Ines' hand is steady; Ines' other hand closes around the edge of the too-small towel; Ines does not pull the towel higher to cover the ridge; Ines lets the towel sit exactly where the towel sits; Ines meets Ines' own eyes in the mirror and stays there;

SFX: **SINGLE EXHALE; DRIP**

**Tone**: A woman meets the thing her body is becoming and decides, at 6:14 AM in a cold room, not to fight it yet.

# Clip #03: Yuna Park — "The Space Between"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: The scene's power lives in what is deliberately withheld — the gap the audience fills themselves.

## [SETTING]
Interior of a late-night highway diner booth, 11 PM, somewhere between two cities that are not named. Window seat. Outside: rain on asphalt, the occasional slow sweep of passing headlights. Inside: a Formica table, a half-eaten plate of fries gone cold, two water glasses sweating rings into paper napkins, a laminated menu standing upright between them like a small wall. Overhead fluorescent hums. The jukebox plays something instrumental and Korean from 1987, barely audible, and neither of them has commented on it.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
Anamorphic 2.39:1, shot on Kodak Vision3 500T, natural window light and practical fluorescents only. Long takes. Static camera or the slowest possible push. Reflections in the window carrying half of each face. Color grade: cool blue exterior, warm amber interior, the line between them running directly through the glass. Sound design: rain, fluorescent buzz, the small clink of ice, the jukebox held at -24dB. No score. Restrained and load-bearing silence.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Hana**: late 30s, hair pulled back, a wedding ring on, coat still on though the booth is warm. Hasn't touched the fries. **Voice Style**: Quiet, unaccented English, Korean underneath when tired. Does not speak in this clip.

**Jun-seo**: late 30s, sweater with a loose cuff, no ring, flew in this morning, eats one fry at a time without looking at it. **Voice Style**: Quiet, Seoul-accented English. Speaks once.

## [SHOT LIST]

**[SHOT 1: MS - EYE LEVEL, STATIC]**

SFX: **RAIN ON ASPHALT; FLUORESCENT HUM; DISTANT KOREAN JUKEBOX BALLAD**

**[SHOT 2: ECU - EYE LEVEL, STATIC]**

Hana's hand rests beside the water glass; Jun-seo's hand rests beside Jun-seo's water glass; between the two hands, four inches of Formica; Jun-seo's hand moves half an inch forward; Jun-seo's hand stops; Jun-seo's hand does not continue; Hana's hand does not move to meet Jun-seo's hand; Hana's hand does not retreat either;

**[SHOT 3: MCU - EYE LEVEL, STATIC]**

Jun-seo looks up for the first time; Jun-seo looks at Hana; Hana does not return the look but Hana's jaw softens almost imperceptibly; Jun-seo speaks once, quietly, in the small voice people use for something they've already decided not to fight for;

**Tone**: Two people who chose different lives sit in a booth not choosing each other again, and the choice that was never made fills the room.

# Clip #04: Léonie Marsh — "The Ambiguity Engine"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: Two contradictory accounts of the same event, both fully supported — the audience decides and the script never does.

## [SETTING]
Interior of a family kitchen at 2 AM, four days after the funeral. Overhead pendant light on a dimmer, set low. A kettle on the stove beginning to tick as it cools. A half-cleared table: three wine glasses, one empty, one half-full with a lipstick mark, one untouched. A child's drawing pinned to the fridge. The back door is slightly ajar onto a dark garden — not enough to be suspicious, enough to be noticed. A baby monitor on the counter emits faint white noise from an upstairs bedroom.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
Observational mid-budget European drama realism. ARRI Alexa, 35mm, prime lenses, soft practical light sources only. The camera refuses to choose a protagonist — coverage is equal, framing is equal, no close-up is granted without its mirror. No score. No sound design flourishes. Audio presence flat and courtroom-clean. Every frame composed so that either person could be the one telling the truth.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Camille**: 44, sister of the deceased, wool cardigan, holding a mug with both hands, tired in a specific way. **Voice Style**: French-accented English, level, conversational, carefully casual.

**Adrien**: 46, husband of the deceased, sleeves rolled, one hand on the table, one hand out of frame under the table. **Voice Style**: French-accented English, measured, slightly too calm.

## [SHOT LIST]

**[SHOT 1: MS - EYE LEVEL, STATIC, TWO-SHOT]**

Camille and Adrien sit on opposite sides of the kitchen table; the composition is perfectly symmetrical; the pendant light falls equally on both faces; Camille sets the mug down; Adrien's hand under the table is not visible, which may or may not be deliberate; the back door's gap is framed between them;

CAMILLE (Conversational, Careful): "I'm only asking because the police will ask.";

SFX: **KETTLE TICK; BABY MONITOR WHITE NOISE**

**[SHOT 2: MCU - EYE LEVEL, STATIC, ON ADRIEN]**

Adrien's face is neither defensive nor performative; Adrien's breath is even; Adrien looks directly at Camille without flinching and also without the kind of directness that performs innocence; Adrien's hand remains under the table; a reading of grief and a reading of calculation are both fully available;

ADRIEN (Measured, Even): "I told you what I told them. She went out. She came back. I was upstairs.";

SFX: **FAINT CREAK OF THE BACK DOOR IN A DRAUGHT**

**[SHOT 3: MCU - EYE LEVEL, STATIC, ON CAMILLE]**

Camille's face mirrors Adrien's in weight and framing; Camille's eyes do not narrow, do not widen; Camille takes one controlled sip from the mug; Camille's next sentence is either a sister protecting her late sister's husband or a sister laying a trap, and the scene declines to indicate which;

CAMILLE (Level, Quiet): "I believe you. I only want to make sure we tell the same story.";

SFX: **KETTLE TICK; UPSTAIRS, A CHILD TURNS OVER IN A BED**

**Tone**: Two survivors build an agreement in a lit kitchen and the agreement is either a kindness or a conspiracy and the script will not, and does not, decide.

# Clip #05: Oskar Brandt — "The Monument Builder"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: A small human story told across decades — the gap between the intimate and the epic IS the meaning.

## [SETTING]
Exterior of a half-finished concrete cultural hall on a hilltop outside a small industrial city, dawn, late autumn, 1974. The structure is massive — raw board-formed concrete, exposed rebar crowning the unfinished parapet, scaffolding on three sides, a crane silent against a pale sky. At the base of the east wall, a single wooden ladder leads to a small mason's alcove. Frost on the concrete. The city below is barely waking: one train, one distant factory whistle. A thermos sits on a folded coat beside a canvas tool bag. The building will outlive everyone who is currently touching it.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
65mm large-format, shot on Kodak 5219 with a vintage Hawk anamorphic, deep focus, composed like architectural photography. Dawn light natural only, no fill. Very long lens for the wide, tight prime for the close. The camera holds. A commissioned orchestral score — low strings, a single clarinet — written for this image, not compiled from library cues. Duration is the argument.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Tomáš**: 54, Czech émigré stonemason, weathered hands, a wool coat too thin for the cold, a pencil behind one ear, a mason's hammer in one hand. Has worked on this building for nine years. **Voice Style**: Czech-accented English, rare and low, uses as few words as possible.

**The Building**: a presence, not a person. A 40-meter concrete mass that Tomáš helped pour, one wall of which he laid by hand.

## [SHOT LIST]

**[SHOT 1: EWS - LOW ANGLE, STATIC, HELD]**

The cultural hall fills three-quarters of the frame against the pale sky; Tomáš is a small dark figure at the base of the east wall, climbing the wooden ladder slowly, one rung at a time; the crane does not move; the city does not move; the frame holds for four full seconds and nothing in it is fast;

SFX: **DISTANT TRAIN; COLD WIND ACROSS CONCRETE; CLARINET HELD NOTE UNDER**

**[SHOT 2: MS - EYE LEVEL, SLOW DOLLY IN]**

Tomáš reaches the alcove and sets one gloved hand flat against the concrete wall Tomáš poured in 1966; Tomáš's hand stays on the wall; Tomáš does not speak; Tomáš's other hand withdraws a small chisel from the tool bag; Tomáš begins, with great care, to carve a letter into the wall's hidden inner face where no future visitor will ever see it;

SFX: **STEEL CHISEL ON CURED CONCRETE — SINGLE TAP, SINGLE TAP, SINGLE TAP**

**[SHOT 3: ECU - EYE LEVEL, STATIC]**

The letter forming is the first letter of a name that is not Tomáš's; the letter is being carved on the side of the wall that will face, once the interior is finished, a service corridor no member of the public will ever enter; Tomáš's breath fogs once in the cold; Tomáš (under breath, to the wall, not to the camera) speaks the only line of the clip;

TOMÁŠ (Low, Czech-Accented, To the Wall): "For you. Not for them.";

SFX: **FINAL TAP; ORCHESTRAL SWELL — LOW STRINGS RISING UNDER A DISTANT FACTORY WHISTLE**

**Tone**: A man who will be forgotten signs his love into a building that will outlast his country, on a wall nobody will ever read, and the scale of the building and the smallness of the gesture are the same argument.

# Clip #06: Tobias Clay — "The Root Doctor"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: Genre as the only language precise enough to tell a cultural truth realism can't hold.

## [SETTING]
Exterior crossroads at dusk, rural Mississippi Delta, 1937. Two red-dirt roads meet beneath a leaning cypress hung with Spanish moss. A wooden juke joint sits fifty yards down one road, warm kerosene light spilling from its doorway, upright piano leaking a slow twelve-bar blues into the humid air. Cicadas. A single kerosene lantern burns at the crossroads itself, set on a stump. Sky the color of a bruise — indigo bleeding into rust. The soil at the crossroads looks darker than the soil around it, as if it has been fed.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
35mm celluloid, Kodak Vision3 500T push-processed, 2.39:1 anamorphic. Deep warm shadows, lantern-glow as practical key, faces half-lit. Color palette: blood-rust, cypress-green, kerosene-amber, indigo dusk. Grain visible. No stylized effects — photographed like Southern Gothic documentary. Diegetic music bleeds across every shot.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Delphine Arceneaux**: Black woman, late 60s, headwrap the color of dried blood, hands scarred from decades of root work, bare feet on red earth. **Voice Style**: Low alto, unhurried, Louisiana Creole inflection, speaks like every word costs her something.

**Young Man (Cornelius)**: Black man, early 20s, Sunday suit too big at the shoulders, guitar case on the ground, sweat on the brow, eyes flickering between Delphine and the dark down the road. **Voice Style**: Nervous tenor, trying to sound grown.

## [SHOT LIST]

**[SHOT 1: ECU - LOW ANGLE, STATIC]**

Delphine's bare foot presses into the red dirt at the crossroads. Dirt darker than it should be. A single drop of sweat falls from frame-top and disappears into the soil;

SFX: **DISTANT BOTTLENECK SLIDE GUITAR, BLUES IN A MINOR KEY — LEAKING FROM THE JUKE JOINT**

**[SHOT 2: MCU - EYE LEVEL, SLOW DOLLY IN]**

Delphine's face half-lit by lantern, eyes on Cornelius. Delphine's hand opens, palm up, empty;

DELPHINE (Low, measured, unhurried): "This ground remembers. You bring a song out here, the ground gon' want to know whose song it was before yours";

SFX: **CICADA DRONE; PIANO CHORD SUSTAINING**

**[SHOT 3: MCU - EYE LEVEL, HANDHELD]**

Cornelius swallows. Cornelius's hand tightens on the guitar case handle. Cornelius looks down the dark road, then back;

CORNELIUS (Nervous, too quick): "I just want to play, ma'am. I just want to be heard";

SFX: **WIND THROUGH CYPRESS — LOW MOAN**

**[SHOT 4: WS - LOW ANGLE, STATIC]**

Wide on the crossroads. Delphine and Cornelius small against the bruised sky. Behind Cornelius, down the dark road, a second figure stands where no figure was a moment ago — unmoving, Sunday-suited, no face visible. The juke joint's blues swells;

DELPHINE (Quieter, like a warning that is already too late): "Somebody already heard you, baby";

SFX: **BLUES PIANO RESOLVES TO THE FIVE CHORD AND HOLDS — UNRESOLVED**

**Tone**: The land is the oldest character in the scene, and the deal at the crossroads was made before anyone arrived — genre as ancestral memory, the blues as both sanctuary and summoning.

# Clip #07: Arthur Price — "The Reluctant Humanist"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: The character's self-narrative is wrong — the story knows them better than they know themselves.

## [SETTING]
Interior, faculty common room of a second-tier New England boarding school, 7:14 on a January morning. Radiator clanking. A long oak table scarred by decades of coffee rings. Leaded windows fogged from the inside. One electric kettle, one chipped Wedgwood teapot, one box of supermarket shortbread open and stale. Green-shaded banker's lamps. A framed 1962 rowing team photograph hangs askew. The smell of instant coffee and wet wool coats on the rack.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
16mm grain, Kodak 250D, natural window light supplemented by tungsten practicals, 1.85:1. Muted palette: tweed brown, radiator green, teabag beige, wool navy. Held handheld but barely — the camera respects the room. No push-ins during dialogue. The actor's face does the work.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Hollis Brenner**: Man, 64, history master, tweed jacket with suede elbows two decades past fashionable, permanent half-sneer, glasses on a cord. **Voice Style**: Dry baritone, over-articulated consonants, pretends every sentence is a minor inconvenience.

**Priya Ramaswamy**: Woman, 23, first-year English teaching fellow, turtleneck and clogs, clutching a mug with both hands, wary. **Voice Style**: Quiet mezzo, Midwestern, picks words carefully, does not laugh at Hollis's jokes.

# Clip #08: Sola Jin — "The World Whisperer"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: One physical rule makes an invisible real-world structure visible — follow one consequence through one life until it breaks something irreplaceable.

## [SETTING]
Interior, a vertical apartment shaft. The world's one rule: sound travels downward only — a word spoken on floor 40 can be heard on floor 12, but nothing spoken below can ever be heard above. The shaft is a single continuous architecture, forty residential balconies stacked open to a central atrium, each balcony railed in perforated brass. Floor numbers painted enormous on the inner wall: 12 at our level, the numbers climbing into vertiginous perspective above. The air is heavy at the bottom — humid, full of the accumulated speech of thirty-eight floors above, a constant indistinct murmur like a river. On floor 12's balcony: a single folding chair, a small paper-wrapped package, a woman. Forty floors up, a man at his railing, barely visible.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
Digital, large-format anamorphic, 2.39:1. Architectural precision. Color palette stratified by altitude: high floors washed in cold daylight (pale blue, bone white), low floors in dim amber sodium, with a visible gradient down the shaft. Shallow depth of field used only to pull focus between altitudes. No hand-held. The world is geometry.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Noor (Floor 12)**: Woman, 34, laborer's coveralls, hair in a practical knot, face upturned throughout, holding a small paper-wrapped package against her chest. **Voice Style**: Not applicable — cannot be heard by the floor above. Mouths words silently.

**Idris (Floor 40)**: Man, 36, thin shirt suited to the cold upper altitude, leaning on the brass railing. **Voice Style**: Warm tenor, unhurried, speaks into the shaft knowing every word will reach her and none of hers will reach him.

## [SHOT LIST]

**[SHOT 1: VWS - WORM'S EYE, STATIC]**

Looking straight up the shaft from floor 12. Forty floors of balconies recede into cold white daylight. A single figure leans at the top railing — Idris, tiny. The murmur of thirty-eight floors of descending speech fills the air. Noor's hand enters frame-bottom, palm up, catching nothing;

SFX: **ACCUMULATED VOCAL MURMUR — RIVER-LIKE, INDISTINCT, THIRTY-EIGHT OVERLAPPING VOICES FALLING**

**[SHOT 2: ECU - EYE LEVEL, STATIC]**

The paper-wrapped package in Noor's hands on floor 12. Noor's thumb presses a crease flat. A tag reads, in handwriting: FLOOR 40, IDRIS. The package is the last one. A small envelope beneath it reads: RETURN TO SENDER — UNDELIVERABLE;

SFX: **PAPER CRINKLE; DISTANT WIND IN THE SHAFT ABOVE**

**[SHOT 3: MCU - HIGH ANGLE, STATIC]**

From above Noor's shoulder, looking down at the package, then up the shaft. Noor's mouth moves — silent — shaping two syllables that will never climb. Noor's chest rises, falls;

SFX: **MURMUR CONTINUES; A SINGLE CLEAR VOICE RESOLVES OUT OF IT FROM ABOVE**

**[SHOT 4: MCU - LOW ANGLE, SLOW DOLLY IN ON NOOR]**

Noor's face upturned, lit pale by the cold daylight falling down the shaft. Idris's voice arrives clean, close, as if spoken at her ear. Noor's eyes close. Noor does not answer, because no answer will ever arrive;

IDRIS (Warm, unhurried, the voice of a man who has never once been answered): "I know you're still there. I can feel the weight of you, down at the bottom of the air";

SFX: **IDRIS'S VOICE — CLOSE, INTIMATE, IMPOSSIBLY CLEAR; MURMUR FALLS AWAY FOR A BEAT; RETURNS**

**Tone**: Love across a one-way physics — the architecture is the heartbreak, and the rule is the relationship.

# Clip #09: Declan Lowe — "The Pressure Cook"

**Duration**: 10 seconds
**Mechanism**: The crew under sustained pressure IS the story — revealed through who covers whose station when it all goes sideways.

## [SETTING]
Interior, the line of a 22-seat Basque pintxos bar in the Mission, 8:47 PM Saturday, mid-second-turn. Six feet of stainless steel between four cooks. Flat-top screaming, plancha smoke, a printer vomiting tickets faster than the expo can tear them, one broken low-boy held shut with a bungee. The overhead pass is nine tickets deep. Someone's AirPod is playing Rosalía through the vent hood rattle. The bar is seven feet from the line — guests can see everything.

## [STYLE REFERENCE]
Digital, Alexa Mini LF, 2x anamorphic, 1.85:1, 180° shutter for motion blur in the hands. Aggressive handheld — the camera is a fifth crew member, shoulder-to-shoulder. Practical kitchen light: fluorescent pass, halogen heat lamps, gas flame under-light. Color: cyan fluorescent crushed against orange flame, stainless reflecting both. Cuts on motion. Overlapping audio, no clean mix.

## [SUBJECTS]
**Mara (Expo)**: Woman, 38, head shaved, burn-scar on the left forearm, ticket-rail commander, reads nine tickets like sheet music. **Voice Style**: Hoarse alto, short syllables, never raises volume — her calm is the weapon.
