# Prestige Period Intrigue

## Slot
Period-set serialized drama (Regency / Edwardian / Victorian / Tudor / Gilded Age / any equivalent stratified historical setting) where the surface is ETIQUETTE — drawing rooms, balls, dressing, dining, calling cards, correspondence — and the engine is POWER, MARRIAGE, INHERITANCE, and FORBIDDEN DESIRE moving underneath. Commercial neighborhood: the Bridgerton / Downton Abbey / The Crown / Wolf Hall / The Gilded Age shelf — costume drama where the manners themselves are weapons, and concealment is the genre's central craft.

## Emotional contract
**Etiquette + concealment + power.** The audience pays for the PLEASURE of watching manners DOUBLE as weapons — for the moment a calling card carries a threat, a dance carries a proposal, a seating chart carries a humiliation. The genre's specific catharsis is the visible movement of power beneath a polite surface. When a feeling is shown, it is shown OBLIQUELY — through a fan, a glove, a refusal to look up. The audience reads what the characters cannot say.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone — every period intrigue uses all three):**
- **The Etiquette As Weapon** — A social gesture (seating, an introduction, a name omitted, a calling card returned, a dance refused) used to wound or block. The recipe's primary unit. Audiences must be TAUGHT the rule so the violation lands.
- **The Concealed Desire / Plot** — Something the surface cannot show is the engine — a love, a child, a bastard, a debt, an illness, a political alliance, a heresy. The recipe spends most of its runtime on what is HIDDEN beneath what is shown.
- **The Ball / Court / Dinner / Season** — A formal SETTING that compresses social tension. The recipe always has one. The Season is calendrical; events happen ON specific nights, in specific rooms.

**Supporting tropes (stack 4-6 per work):**
- **The Gossip Channel** — A named in-world conduit of information — Lady Whistledown's broadsheet, the society column, the housekeeper's network, court whispers. The audience hears what the characters hear, sometimes more.
- **The Calling Card / Invitation Refused** — Social violence through paper. The card sent, the card returned, the card never extended. The recipe's most concentrated form.
- **The Letter Discovered** — Correspondence found, intercepted, read, kept. The recipe's primary evidence. Often the third-act pivot.
- **The Garden / The Conservatory / The Library** — Sanctioned places where unsanctioned conversations happen. Each setting has rules about what can be said there.
- **The Marriage Market** — The genre's economic substrate. Daughters, sons, dowries, inheritance, succession. The recipe makes the financial visible; love is layered on top of the spreadsheet.
- **The Below-Stairs Parallel** — A servant-class plot that mirrors or undercuts an upstairs plot. Two engagements at the same time; the housemaid's grief running under the lady's.
- **The Walk** — Two characters walking and talking in a controlled space (the gardens, the long gallery, the promenade). The recipe's primary dialogue form; the walk is the setting.
- **The Reveal of the Hidden Birth** — Parentage, legitimacy, bastardy, mixed heritage. The recipe loves a secret birth. Often discovered through a letter or a servant's memory.
- **The Court Politics Subplot** — A national-political plot (war, succession, parliamentary vote, papal bull, treaty) running in parallel to the personal. Wolf Hall and The Crown live here.
- **The Forbidden Liaison Caught** — The Concealed Desire risks exposure — a kiss seen, a letter intercepted, a midnight visit. The third-act pivot in many works.
- **The Fortune Lost / Made** — A financial reversal — the inherited estate gone, the new-money family bought in, the mortgage called. The recipe's stakes are material as well as romantic.
- **The Older-Woman Power Center** — Dowager / queen mother / matron / Mrs. Astor — a woman whose age has converted her into power. The recipe always has one; she is sometimes the Arbiter, sometimes a counterweight to the Arbiter.
- **The Foreigner Arrives** — Someone from outside the System enters — colonial cousin, French émigré, American heiress, Spanish ambassador, Turkish guest. Their unfamiliarity with the rules drives plot.
- **The Costume Reveal** — A character appears in an unexpected costume — mourning, full regalia, a forbidden color, an unmasked appearance at a masque. The recipe makes the visual carry plot.
- **The Quiet Letter Burned** — A character DESTROYS evidence — a love letter, a will, a confession. The recipe permits its protagonists to choose concealment.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't have characters speak their feelings in plain modern language. The recipe is built on the OBLIQUE — a glove, a fan, a refusal to look up.
- Don't have the System bend casually. The rules must hold; the violation must cost. A duchess who marries the gardener without scandal belongs in a different recipe.
- Don't have the servants exist only as scenery. The Below-Stairs Parallel is non-negotiable; the recipe acknowledges the labor that sustains the surface.
- Don't make the Arbiter a cardboard villain. The recipe's Arbiters have coherent worldviews about why the System should hold; the audience should partly agree.
- Don't anachronize the politics flatly. The recipe is unembarrassed about hierarchies, marriages-of-convenience, inheritance laws — it shows them, and lets the audience read them. Imposing 21st-century clean morality on every character flattens the recipe.
- Don't resolve the political and personal in the same beat. The recipe keeps the two registers separate; they intersect but do not merge.
- Don't have the Confidant be a comic prop. Their interpretive labor is the audience's eye into the world.
- Don't film the world like it's contemporary. The recipe is aesthetically saturated — fabric, light, room geography, table settings. The visual IS the world.

## Conformity rules
- The setting is HISTORICAL. The recipe runs on world-rules the audience must be taught. Modern-dress versions belong in a different recipe.
- Etiquette must function as weapon and currency. The audience must be TAUGHT the rule so the violation lands. Showing the rule is part of the recipe's pedagogy.
- The Below-Stairs Parallel is non-negotiable. The labor that sustains the surface must be visible to the audience.
- The Concealed Desire / Plot drives the season. What is hidden must be the engine; what is shown must be the surface.
- The System must hold. Casual violations without cost collapse the recipe. The rules must matter, even if specific characters break them.
- The Arbiter and the Institution must be DIFFERENT roles. Social gates and political power do not collapse into one figure.
- The visual is structural. Costume, room, light, fabric, season, weather — the recipe is aesthetically saturated.
- Both the romantic and the political must run. The recipe has a marriage-market spine AND a court-politics spine; both must be visible.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Era and country — Regency England, Edwardian England, Tudor court, Gilded Age New York, 18th-century France, Russian Imperial, Scottish Highland (Outlander). Open.
- Tonal register — Bridgerton-romp, Downton-warm, Crown-measured, Wolf Hall-cold, Buccaneers-naive-American, Great-anachronistic-comic, North & South-industrial-romantic.
- The Striver's specific marginalization — younger daughter (Sense and Sensibility), wrong-class heiress (The Buccaneers, Gilded Age), wrong-race woman (Belle, Bridgerton), wrong-politics heir (Wolf Hall), wrong-nationality bride (Outlander).
- The Forbidden Object's specific unsuitability — class, race, sex, religion, nationality, politics, prior marriage, illness, money.
- The Gossip Channel's medium — broadsheet (Bridgerton), letters (P&P), society column (Gilded Age), court whispers (Wolf Hall, The Crown).
- Whether the recipe is comedic, romantic, tragic, or institutional in its primary register.
- The proportion of court politics to marriage market — Wolf Hall is mostly politics; Bridgerton is mostly marriage; Downton splits.
- The Below-Stairs cast's prominence — Downton makes them co-equal; Bridgerton sketches them; Wolf Hall barely shows them.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the etiquette answer arms the Rival; the letter route creates evidence; the political petition exposes the liaison; the settlement clause defines the closing freedom) — every rule, token, route, petition, and clause varies freely.

## Caveats
- The genre's most common failure is FLATTENING the manners into modern dialogue. If characters speak their feelings plainly, the recipe collapses; the entire engine is the OBLIQUE.
- The second-most-common failure is breaking the System for the protagonist without cost. The recipe's pleasure is watching a real System constrain real people. The Striver's wins must be paid for, even if the payment is private.
- The third common failure is treating servants as scenery or props. The Below-Stairs Parallel is part of the recipe; without it the work reads as period romance, not period intrigue.
- The recipe's politics can read uncomfortably to modern audiences. The recipe trusts the audience to read the System critically WITHOUT having every character voice that critique in modern terms. Anachronistic moralizing flattens the recipe; show the System, let the audience judge.
- Tonal register dial is wide — Bridgerton-romp and Wolf Hall-cold are the same recipe at opposite extremities. Same beats, different intensities of manners-as-weapon and same proportions of personal-to-political.
- The recipe shades into adjacent slots: operatic_melodrama when declarations replace the oblique; erotic_tension_slow_approach when restraint replaces concealment-with-plot; forced_together_romance when the proximity-engine takes over from the System-engine. Hold the LINE on: etiquette is weapon, the Concealed drives, the Season has a calendar.
