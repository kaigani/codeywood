# Forced Together Romance

## Slot
Romantic comedy / romantic drama where two people who don't want each other are LOCKED into close proximity by external constraint (job, weather, family, fake-marriage, mistaken identity, shared apartment, blackout, blizzard, road trip, work assignment) and the friction of forced contact becomes desire. Commercial neighborhood: the When Harry Met Sally / Pride and Prejudice / The Proposal shelf — proximity-engine romance where the EXTERNAL CONSTRAINT is the engine, not chosen attraction.

## Emotional contract
**Friction + proximity + dissolve.** The audience pays for the gradual erosion of two people's reasons not to want each other. Pleasure is in the SHRINKING DISTANCE — the moment hostility curdles into awareness, awareness into want, want into terror of admitting it, admission into the kiss they spent the whole season earning.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone — every Forced Together Romance turns on one or both):**
- **Forced Proximity** — External constraint locks them into the same physical or social space they cannot exit. The constraint must be plausible, absolute for its duration, and impossible to talk their way out of. Without the constraint, the resistance wins; with it, the dissolve becomes inevitable.
- **Enemies / Wrong-Type to Lovers** — Stated incompatibility at the start. The audience must believe the leads MEAN their resistance. The hate or condescension is real. The dissolve is earned by friction, not handed by chemistry.

**Supporting tropes (stack 3-6 per work):**
- **One Bed** — Constraint forces shared sleeping arrangement (hotel, cabin, inherited cottage, train compartment). The most concentrated dose of physical proximity in the recipe.
- **Fake Relationship** — They pretend to be together for an external audience (family wedding, work event, immigration). Pretending requires touching. Touching dismantles resistance.
- **Mistaken Identity** — One knows something the other doesn't (online vs in-person, alias, pseudonym, hidden role). The reveal must hurt because the connection was real before the truth was known.
- **The Bicker** — Witty hostile dialogue as the courtship's primary form. The fight IS the foreplay. The audience watches the wordcount of their exchanges grow.
- **The Reluctant Drive** — Road trip, long flight, snowed-in journey; uninterrupted forced talk. The container that compresses weeks of getting-to-know into hours.
- **The Almost-Kiss** — Pulled apart by an interruption (phone, third party, sudden cold feet); the audience screams. Use at least one before the real kiss.
- **The Wrong Fiancé / Wedding Crash** — There's an upcoming marriage to the Plausible Option, and the Counterpart's late-act intervention or self-discovery breaks it.
- **The Big Gesture** — Late-act public declaration; running through the airport, the rain, the wedding aisle, the office. Earned by the smallness of all the private moments before.
- **The Letter / Voicemail / Email** — Asynchronous declaration that does work in-person dialogue can't; You've Got Mail, Persuasion's letter, the modern text bubble.
- **Banter as Foreplay** — Verbal sparring in scenes that have nothing romantic on their surface. The audience reads what the characters don't yet feel.
- **The Confidant Confession** — The Resister tells the Confidant something true about the Counterpart, and the Confidant says the line that becomes the audience's permission to root.
- **The Holiday Container** — The constraint is geographic AND temporal — Christmas, summer vacation, work retreat, family reunion. Compressed time, heightened stakes.
- **Class Friction** — The "wrong type" is coded by class, education, accent, profession. Pride and Prejudice's engine still works.
- **The Mortifying Misunderstanding** — A scene the Resister cannot live down (the diary read aloud, the voicemail overheard, the rant misdirected). Forces a vulnerability they wouldn't have chosen.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't have either lead's resistance be FAKE from the start — the audience must believe they mean it, or the dissolve is empty.
- Don't have the constraint dissolve early. If they could walk away in episode 4, the recipe collapses. Keep them locked in.
- Don't humiliate the Plausible Option to make the Counterpart look better. The genre is gentle to the rejected — it's the Resister's growth that ends the engagement, not the Plausible Option being secretly terrible.
- Don't resolve the Big Misunderstanding through dialogue alone. The recipe needs ACTION — a gesture, a journey, a public declaration.
- Don't have the leads sleep together early. The genre's pleasure is the held-off; the consummation lands late in the spine, not at the midpoint.
- Don't have either lead's flaw be cured by the other. They each do their own work; the romance is the witness, not the therapy.
- Don't strand the Confidant — they need a small life arc of their own, even if it's just a B-plot reset.

## Conformity rules
- The constraint must be EXTERNAL and ABSOLUTE for its duration. If the leads could walk away in episode 3, it's not the genre.
- Both leads must have a stated, plausible reason to NOT want this. The friction must register as real to the audience, not performed.
- The Plausible Option must be a real character, not a strawman. The genre is generous to the rejected.
- The dissolve must be SLOW. Bicker → reluctant respect → glimpse of the real person → almost-kiss → real kiss → admission. No casual flips.
- A Confidant must exist and must be allowed to be right before the leads are.
- The genre is ROMANTIC. Both leads survive, both leads choose each other, the audience leaves warmer than they arrived.
- The Big Gesture is non-negotiable. The genre is built around the public declaration; do it.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- The External Constraint — job, weather, fake engagement, inheritance, road trip, family wedding, immigration paperwork, shared apartment, work retreat. Invent every time.
- Tonal register — screwball (Bringing Up Baby, Proposal), classic comedy (You've Got Mail, While You Were Sleeping), prestige slow-burn (Persuasion, Lake House), confessional warmth (Bridget Jones), political-comedic (Long Shot).
- Class friction's specific axis — money, education, region, accent, profession, age.
- The Almost-Kiss interrupter — phone, child, third party, weather, sudden cold feet.
- The Big Gesture's container — airport, wedding, office, family dinner, public speech, online message.
- Whether the leads sleep together before the admission (mid-tonal: yes if comedic, no if prestige).
- The Plausible Option's exit register — gracious (Persuasion), comedic (Proposal), reluctant (Bridget Jones).
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the forced bargain creates household intimacy; denial recruits the Plausible Option and later wounds the Counterpart; the final gesture voluntarily reuses what proximity established) — every constraint, insult, private object, misunderstanding, and gesture varies freely.

## Caveats
- The genre's most common failure is making the constraint feel CHOSEN. If either lead could leave and doesn't, the audience reads it as both already wanting each other, and the recipe collapses into a slower drama. The constraint must override their stated wills. (Now encoded in the wiring: E1's bargain, E2's receipt, and E9's dissolution.)
- The second-most-common failure is making the Counterpart secretly perfect from episode 1 — competent, witty, kind, beautiful, attentive. If the Resister's resistance has no surface justification, the audience pulls ahead of the story and waits for the leads to catch up. Both leads must remain partly correct about each other through episode 6. (Now encoded in the wiring: E2's mutual insult and E7's proud retreat.)
- The Plausible Option problem: making them villainous to make the breakup easier is the lazy fix. Keep them sympathetic; the Resister's growth ends the engagement, not the Plausible Option's failings.
- Tonal range is wide (screwball ↔ prestige slow-burn). Same recipe, different dials — Bringing Up Baby and Persuasion are the same engine at opposite speeds.
- This is a 10-episode TV recipe. For 90-min film, compress to 8 beats by merging episodes 2-3 (proximity detail) and 4-5 (Plausible Option visit).
