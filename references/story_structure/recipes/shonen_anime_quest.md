# Shonen Anime Quest (Friendship-Powered Escalation)

## Slot
Serialized anime (or manga-adapted) where a young protagonist pursues a long-arc goal — power, vengeance, mastery, a missing brother, a king's title — and the engine is the bond with a small core team whose loyalty escalates alongside the power tier. Each arc raises the ceiling: stronger enemies, deeper rules of the system, harder costs. The catharsis is earned-power-through-bond.

## Corpus
- *One Piece* (Oda / Toei)
- *Naruto / Naruto Shippuden* (Kishimoto / Pierrot)
- *Demon Slayer* (Gotouge / ufotable)
- *My Hero Academia* (Horikoshi / Bones)
- *Jujutsu Kaisen* (Akutami / MAPPA)
- *Hunter x Hunter* (Togashi / Madhouse)
- *Bleach* (Kubo / Pierrot)
- *Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood* (Arakawa / Bones)
- *Yu Yu Hakusho* (Togashi / Pierrot)
- *Black Clover* (Tabata / Pierrot)
- *Chainsaw Man* (Fujimoto / MAPPA)
- *Dragon Ball Z* (Toriyama / Toei)
- *Soul Eater* (Ohkubo / Bones)
- *Attack on Titan* (lean — has shonen DNA, dark register)
- *Spy x Family* (lean — found-family chassis without combat tier)

(Tonal range: earnest-Demon Slayer through bombastic-DBZ through scrappy-MHA to satirical-Chainsaw Man. Same engine — bond + escalation + earned-power — at registers from sincere to nihilist-comedy.)

## Emotional contract
**Friendship + escalation + earned-power.** The viewer falls in love with the core trio (or small crew) early; each arc raises the ceiling — new enemies, harder rules, deeper stakes — and the bond holds (or breaks-and-reforges) at every escalation. Catharsis is the power-up landing because the bond demanded it, not because it was time.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (the engine):**
- **The Power System Has Tiers.** The world's combat / magic / ability system has explicit, named, escalating tiers — Genin/Chunin/Jonin, Hashira/Demon ranks, Curse grades, Quirks, Bankai/Shikai. The audience tracks the protagonist's tier across arcs.
- **The Friendship Power-Up.** When the team is in danger, the Protagonist unlocks the next tier — but the unlock is triggered by a moment of bond (rage at a friend's harm, resolve to protect, the team's faith). Not training-floor alone. The bond IS the unlock.
- **The Long-Arc Goal.** A single-sentence goal stated in episode 1 ("become Hokage," "be Pirate King," "kill all demons"). The audience tracks every arc as movement toward this goal.
- **The Tournament Arc.** At least once, the team enters a structured contest (Chunin Exam, Hunter Exam, U.A. Sports Festival, the World Tournament). All major characters compete; tiers are calibrated; the audience gets a chessboard.
- **The Backstory Flashback.** Every named villain (and most named teammates) gets a substantial backstory flashback that contextualizes their flaw and motive. The audience must understand why they fight before they fall.

**Supporting tropes (stack 5-10 per arc):**
- **The Training Montage.** A defined period off-screen-collapsed where the Protagonist learns the next technique. Often with a new mentor figure.
- **The Named Technique.** Every signature move has a name shouted at the moment of execution. Rasengan, Getsuga Tensho, Detroit Smash, Hollow Purple. Naming is the cathartic act.
- **The Power-Up Reveal.** A new tier or technique is shown in the middle of a fight, ideally interrupting an enemy's monologue. The audience cheers.
- **The Sacrifice Save.** A teammate takes a fatal (or near-fatal) hit to save another; the Protagonist's grief unlocks the next tier.
- **The Redemption-Through-Backstory.** A villain's backstory humanizes them; sometimes they switch sides; sometimes they die understood.
- **The Mentor's Death.** The Mentor dies (often in a mid-series climactic arc). The Protagonist is rebuilt around the loss.
- **The Found Family.** The team is explicitly framed as family-by-choice; non-blood bonds outrank blood ties. Recurring in dialogue and in formal scenes (meals, group photos, sworn oaths).
- **The Filler Arc / Joy Episode.** A low-stakes episode of comedy, beach, festival, hot springs. Reaffirms the bond before the next escalation. Audience permission to love these people.
- **The Power Awakening.** A moment where the Protagonist's true ability (often a sealed-away or inherited power) manifests. Visual transformation; new color palette; the world adjusts to the new tier.
- **The Villain's Speech.** Apex villains deliver a worldview monologue mid-fight. The team interrupts with action. The genre's argument-via-fist.
- **The Time Skip.** A multi-month or multi-year gap; characters return aged-up, more trained, harder. Often midway through the series.
- **The Final Form.** Last-arc transformation that surpasses everything previously shown. Visible (hair color, eye color, body change, aura). Audience knows: this is the ceiling.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't let the Protagonist win solo against the Final Villain. The bond closes the deal; the team's combined effort (not literal combination, but coordinated) is the catharsis.
- Don't have the team abandon the Protagonist permanently. Temporary fracture is canon; permanent abandonment violates the contract.
- Don't power-up without bond. Pure training-floor unlocks read empty. The friendship-power-up is non-negotiable.
- Don't kill the Protagonist permanently mid-series. Apparent deaths followed by Power Awakening returns are canonical; permanent death is a different genre.
- Don't resolve the Rival as a clean enemy. The Rival's arc must remain in tension — either slowly redeemed or carrying the redemption-tease through the series.
- Don't explain every system rule. The power system must retain mystery — there's always a deeper tier, an older technique, a forgotten clan.
- Don't end with the long-arc goal unaddressed. Either the Protagonist achieves it, achieves something larger that supersedes it, or the goal is openly transformed.

## Conformity rules
- The power system must have explicit, named, escalating tiers that the audience can track. Vague magic is a different recipe.
- The Friendship Power-Up must trigger at least once per major arc — bond-pressure precedes the unlock, not training alone.
- The Found Family must be explicit. Dialogue, formal scenes (meals, oaths, photographs) name the team as family-by-choice.
- Named techniques are non-negotiable. Every signature move is shouted at execution. Audiences chant these names.
- The Rival Genius must hold the Protagonist's mirror through the series. Their reconciliation/divergence is the second-strongest emotional arc.
- Backstory flashbacks for named villains are mandatory. Humanizing the antagonist before defeat is genre-floor.
- The long-arc goal stated in episode 1 must remain the audience's tracker through every arc.
- Joy beats (low-stakes filler / festival / meal episodes) must be present. Constant escalation without joy reads grim — the genre needs the reaffirmation to make the escalation cost.
- The ending of a season must visibly raise the ceiling — new tier shown, new world-rule revealed, new arc set up. Closure-without-escalation undercuts the engine.
- Shonen has well-known issues with the Heart role (often a female teammate sidelined as the series goes on). The recipe assumes the modern-corrective frame — the Heart's specialization must remain load-bearing, not decorative. Watch for the "Sakura problem."
- Project-specific note: the recipe is FLOOR. Persona-driven polemic colors the world (which power-system, which long-arc goal, what kind of system the team opposes) but cannot remove the bond-engine, the tier escalation, or the named-technique grammar.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Setting layer — feudal-fantasy (Demon Slayer, Naruto), modern-supernatural (JJK, Bleach, Chainsaw Man), academy (MHA, HxH partial), pirate world (One Piece), industrial-magic (FMA), post-apocalyptic (AoT). All canonical.
- Power-system flavor — chakra (Naruto), breathing-styles (Demon Slayer), Quirks (MHA), Cursed Energy (JJK), Nen (HxH), alchemy (FMA), Devil Fruits (One Piece). Each generates different ceiling-shape.
- Tonal register — earnest (Demon Slayer, MHA), bombastic (DBZ, Bleach), grim-but-still-shonen (AoT, Chainsaw Man), scrappy-comic (One Piece, Spy x Family). The dial is sincerity vs satire.
- Protagonist temperament — loud (Naruto, Luffy, Deku), quiet-determined (Tanjiro, Edward), nihilist-comedy (Denji), cold-prodigy (Gon, sometimes). All canonical; each generates different team dynamics.
- Long-arc goal flavor — political-status (Hokage, Pirate King, Hashira), familial (Edward / Al, brother's body), eradication (kill all demons, beat All For One), personal (find dad, kill the man who killed family). Pick the texture.
- Team size — duo (Edward/Al), trio (Team 7), quartet (Demon Slayer pillars-arc), crew (One Piece full Strawhats). Larger teams require longer runtime.
- Whether the Mentor survives — usually doesn't (Jiraiya, the Hashira mentors, Gin in some readings). Rare to keep the mentor alive past the mid-series climax.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the first tier consumes the bond, the public confrontation consumes the mark and the training vow, and the climax consumes the oath, the scars, the lesson, and the rescue choice) — the CONTENT of every edge varies freely: which power system, whose blood secret, what the shortcut costs, what form the bond finally takes.

## Caveats
- The genre is volume-tolerant. A 100-episode shonen and a 24-episode shonen-compressed both work IF the beat spine compresses proportionally. Don't strip the spine — strip the filler arcs.
- This recipe overlaps with shojo at the edges (focus on bond), with seinen (deeper register), and with isekai (transported-world variant). Distinguish by the engine: shonen runs on friendship + escalation + earned-power. Strip any of those and it's a different recipe.
- Common failure mode: power escalation without bond escalation. If the tiers go up but the team's intimacy stays flat, the engine breaks. The Friendship Power-Up must REQUIRE the bond. (Now encoded in the wiring: E2-team-bond → E3-named-technique → E7's public win → E10's coordinated finish.)
- Common failure mode: making the Rival Genius a flat villain. The Rival is the genre's secret weapon — their unresolved tension drives audience investment harder than the Final Villain. (Now encoded in the wiring: E2-rival-wound → E4's failed solo duel → E10's earned trust.)
- Common failure mode: Mentor dies but is then forgotten. The death must echo — the Protagonist's later choices must visibly reference what was lost. (Now encoded in the wiring: E5-master-debt → E8-fallen-master → E10's use of the last lesson.)
- Common failure mode: skipping Joy beats. Audiences need the festival / meal / banter to make the next escalation hurt. (Now encoded in the wiring: E2's meal, E6's warm training beat, and E10's joy coda.)
