# Lone Survival (Wilderness + Resourcefulness + Endurance)

## Slot
Serialized survival where a single protagonist is dropped into or trapped in a hostile natural environment — sea, mountain, forest, desert, ice, gorge — and must endure long enough to either be rescued, escape, or reach a destination. The antagonist is the landscape: weather, terrain, animals, scarcity, and the protagonist's own deteriorating mind and body. Other humans, if present, are peripheral — the recipe is about a person alone with the world. Pays in earned competence, accumulated loss, and the texture of attrition.

## Corpus

- *The Revenant* (Iñárritu)
- *127 Hours* (Boyle)
- *Cast Away* (Zemeckis)
- *Into the Wild* (Penn / Krakauer)
- *Jeremiah Johnson* (Pollack)
- *The Grey* (Carnahan)
- *Touching the Void* (Macdonald)
- *Arctic* (Penna)
- *The Way Back* (Weir)
- *Tracks* (Curran)
- *Wild* (Vallée / Strayed)
- *Alone* (History Channel TV)
- *Yellowjackets* (partial, wilderness segments)
- *Mountain Men* (History Channel TV)

(Tonal range: All Is Lost's minimalist-Bressonian and Arctic's near-silent through 127 Hours' kinetic-prestige and Tracks' meditative to The Revenant's lush-prestige and Alone's stripped reality-TV. Same recipe — one body against a landscape, attrition as the engine, competence as the only weapon.)

## Emotional contract
**Endurance + ingenuity + ache.** The audience watches a single protagonist negotiate, hour by hour and task by task, with a world that does not care. The cocktail is admiration earned through small competences, dread accumulated through small mistakes, and the long ache of solitude. Catharsis is survival (or dignified failure), but always paid for in named cost — fingers, friends, faith, weight, the version of the self who entered the wilderness.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Inciting Misfortune** — Specific named event that starts the survival sequence — boat hull punctured, rope cut, plane crashed, partner dead, ankle broken, arm pinned, group separated. Single event, clear cause, irreversible. Episode 1 act-out.
- **The Inventory** — Survivor counts what they have — food, water, fuel, tools, daylight, distance. Done aloud or visually; repeated through the season. The audience runs the math with them.
- **The Task Sequence** — Granular procedural work — building shelter, making fire, treating a wound, fishing, navigating, signaling. The show's primary content. Competence shown step-by-step.
- **The Setback** — A named loss — wound infected, fire put out by rain, supplies destroyed by animal, navigation wrong by a day, tool broken. Wipes progress; resets the clock. Required at least three times.
- **The Threshold of Surrender** — A late-season beat where the protagonist sits down and considers stopping. Not melodramatic — quiet, exhausted. Either passes through and continues, or the surrender becomes the ending. Genre's emotional center.

**Supporting tropes:**
- **The Voiceover / Imagined Dialogue** — Protagonist speaks to the Absent Other, to themselves, or to the Companion Object. The show's main vehicle for interiority when there is no one to talk to.
- **The Journal** — Writing, scratching, marking days; physical record. Acts as exposition vehicle and time-marker.
- **The Forecast** — Weather, light, season changes telegraphed — clouds, animal behavior, frost, fading sun. The Landscape's signals.
- **The Improvised Tool** — Survivor makes something from scavenged materials — splint, snare, fishing line, sled, shelter. Earned competence, visible.
- **The Wound** — A specific injury — cut, break, frostbite, bite, burn — that requires ongoing management. Becomes a clock and a constant cost.
- **The Hallucination / Mirage** — Late-stage, the protagonist sees or hears what is not there — a person, a meal, a building, a voice. The mind's failure shown as image.
- **The Animal Encounter** — Predator scene (bear, wolf, shark, snake) where the protagonist must read the animal's behavior. Not always fatal; tension architecture.
- **The Wrong Choice at the Fork** — Geographic or strategic decision that turns out to be wrong by a day or a mile. Marks attrition's accumulation.
- **The Single Successful Hunt / Catch** — A scene of food obtained — a fish, a rabbit, a bird. Brief joy; the audience exhales with the protagonist.
- **The Lost Companion Object** — Tool, dog, horse, talisman that is lost or breaks. Mourned briefly; protagonist continues.
- **The Found Trace** — Evidence of other humans — abandoned shelter, old fire, footprints, a road, smoke. Hope renewed or extended.
- **The Rescue That Misses** — Plane overhead, ship passing, vehicle on the ridge — that does not see the protagonist. The Landscape's cruelty made specific.
- **The Counting of Days** — Visible mark of time — journal entry, scratch on a beam, season change. Endurance made readable.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't bring a major secondary character into the wilderness. The recipe requires solitude. If a buddy survives alongside the protagonist, it's an adventure film — different genre.
- Don't give the protagonist a superpower or special training that trivializes the landscape. Bear Grylls cartoon-competence kills the recipe. Mistakes must be possible, frequent, costly.
- Don't introduce a human antagonist as primary. The Landscape IS the antagonist. Human hostility belongs to a different recipe.
- Don't let the protagonist be saved by a coincidence they didn't earn. Either they reach the destination by their own attrition / ingenuity, or they fail, or they are rescued by a process the audience saw coming.
- Don't end with the protagonist physically and psychologically unchanged. Loss is the genre's tax — fingers, weight, a friend, faith, the prior self.
- Don't keep the inventory off-screen. The audience must know what the protagonist has. Suspense requires legible scarcity.
- Don't skip the Task Sequence. Procedural texture is the genre. If shelter-building and fire-making and navigation happen offscreen, the recipe collapses to montage.

## Conformity rules
- The protagonist must be alone for the majority of the season. Briefly Encountered figures are allowed; a sustained second co-survivor is the wrong genre.
- The Landscape must be NAMED and SPECIFIC — sea, gorge, ice, canyon, taiga, desert. Generic "wilderness" undermines the recipe.
- Inventory must be visible — the audience runs the math with the protagonist. Hidden scarcity kills suspense.
- Task Sequence must be shown in procedural detail — shelter, fire, water, navigation, wound care. Montage is the wrong genre.
- At least three Setbacks must occur — attrition is the engine.
- The protagonist must change physically AND psychologically by the end. No-cost survival is the wrong genre.
- The Antagonist is the Landscape. No human nemesis. Briefly Encountered figures may be hostile, but they cannot be the throughline.
- The Memory / Absent Other must be present — interiority requires a referent. Pure silence with no inner monologue belongs to art-cinema variant; even then, an artifact (photograph, journal, voiceover) is required.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Landscape type — sea (All Is Lost, Cast Away), mountain (Touching the Void, Everest), ice (Arctic, The Grey), desert (Tracks), forest (Into the Wild, The Revenant), gorge (127 Hours). Each shifts task grammar.
- Era — contemporary default; historical (Jeremiah Johnson, The Revenant); near-future possible.
- Reason for being alone — accident (All Is Lost, Arctic, 127 Hours), expedition gone wrong (Touching the Void, The Revenant), chosen journey (Into the Wild, Wild, Tracks), exile (Jeremiah Johnson), reality-TV contract (Alone).
- Tonal register — minimalist-Bressonian, lush-prestige, kinetic-prestige, meditative, reality-stripped. Recipe constant.
- Outcome — rescued, arrived, failed-with-dignity, ambiguous. All valid; chose deliberately.
- Whether the Memory / Absent Other is reconciled, sharpened, or remains open. The interiority's resolution is a register dial.
- Whether the Companion Object is animate (dog, horse) or inanimate (volleyball, knife, watch). Animate enables a second-character relationship; inanimate keeps the recipe pure-solitary.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the wound must tax the body, the setback must burn a resource, and the ending must consume the vow, the miss, and the final push) — the CONTENT of every edge varies freely: which tool breaks, what trace is found, how the body fails, what peace costs.

## Caveats
- Register dial: minimalist-Bressonian (All Is Lost, Arctic) cuts nearly all dialogue and runs almost pure-procedural; lush-prestige (The Revenant) overlays a vengeance plot but keeps the Landscape primary; reality-TV (Alone) reduces to inventory + task + diary. Same recipe.
- Genre proximity: adventure has a goal beyond survival (treasure, mission); lone survival has only the next hour. Yellowjackets uses partial wilderness segments inside a larger frame — extracts the recipe selectively.
- TV expansion challenge: 10-episode pacing requires the season's geography to be large enough — a multi-day storm, a long traverse, a months-long stranding. Short-incident films (127 Hours, Arctic) require expansion across more terrain or more flashback content; the recipe scales but pacing is the craft problem.
- Common failure mode: a buddy. Lone survival cannot sustain a second survivor as throughline. If a partner stays alive across the season the genre slides into adventure / survival-buddy. The Briefly Encountered are the exception; sustained company is not.
- Common failure mode: action-hero competence. Bear Grylls cartoon survival kills the recipe. Mistakes, infections, failed fires, lost calories, wrong choices — required.
- Common failure mode: no interiority. Watching a body do tasks without a Memory / Absent Other to think toward is a documentary, not lone survival drama. The recipe needs the Absent Other.
- Common failure mode: rescue by coincidence. The protagonist must earn arrival OR fail meaningfully. Deus ex machina kills the genre.
