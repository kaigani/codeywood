# Noir Detective

## Slot
Prestige and procedural crime where an INVESTIGATOR with a moral wound walks into a CORRUPTION (institutional, familial, civic) that everyone around them benefits from concealing, and is forced into a COMPROMISE they can't fully undo. The case closes; the investigator does not heal.

## Corpus
- *Chinatown* (Polanski)
- *The Long Goodbye* (Altman)
- *True Detective* S1 (Pizzolatto / Fukunaga)
- *Mare of Easttown* (HBO)
- *Better Call Saul* (AMC — noir-by-erosion)
- *The Wire* (HBO — institutional noir)
- *Bosch* (Amazon)
- *Cardinal* (CTV)
- *The Bridge* — Bron/Broen (DK/SE)
- *Spiral* / *Engrenages* (France 2)
- *Sharp Objects* (HBO)
- *Top of the Lake* (Campion)
- *Happy Valley* (BBC)
- *L.A. Confidential* (Hanson)

(Tonal range: Bosch's bone-dry procedural through Mare of Easttown's working-town grief to True Detective S1's metaphysical-Southern dread. Same recipe — corruption + compromise — opposite registers.)

## Emotional contract
**Decay + compulsion + complicity.** The audience watches a wounded professional be unable to let the case go, knowing the closer they get the more of themselves they'll have to spend, and feels the queasy pull of wanting them to win even though winning means breaking something they swore to protect.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Prior Case** — An old failure the Investigator carries; surfaces in dreams, photos, a name they won't say. The wound is the engine; the new case is the wound's echo.
- **The Walled Town** — The setting (city block, county, precinct, family) is small enough that everyone is implicated. There is no outside to escape to.
- **The Compromise** — The third-act act the Investigator commits that closes the case but breaks their oath (planting evidence, killing without warrant, burying a tape, sleeping with the source, letting the Patriarch walk). Non-negotiable.
- **The Quiet Boss** — The corruption is held by a single named person who never raises their voice. Power is presented through reduction, not menace.

**Supporting tropes (stack 3-6 per work):**
- **The Bottle / The Pills** — A visible self-medication marker. Not symbolic; behavioral. The audience knows the wound is active when the cap comes off.
- **The Photo Wall** — Pinned strings of evidence in a private room (garage, hotel, attic). The Investigator's interior life made physical.
- **The Dead Partner Echo** — A name spoken once, a photograph passed, a watch worn. The Prior Case has a face.
- **The Wife / Ex Who Knows** — A spouse or former partner who reads the Investigator's compulsion before the audience does. Usually leaves between Episodes 5 and 8.
- **The Press Leak** — Someone (the Investigator, the Foil, the Source) feeds a reporter when the Institution shuts down. The leak has consequences.
- **The Bar at 2 AM** — A nocturnal third place where the Investigator gets the conversation that breaks the case. Bar, diner, hotel lobby, idling car.
- **Forensic vs Vibe Split** — One scene per episode where the lab evidence and the Investigator's instinct disagree. The instinct is right; the cost of trusting it is shown.
- **The Body in Water** — Lake, harbor, drainage canal, ditch. The land-water boundary is where bodies surface.
- **The Politician's Lunch** — A scene where a powerful figure invites the Investigator to a public meal as a soft threat. Coffee, golf, dinner. Civility weaponized.
- **The Suspect Who's Sympathetic** — One suspect the Investigator wants to be guilty (because clean) and one they don't want to be guilty (because painful). The painful one is.
- **The Confession Without Closure** — The killer admits it and the audience still feels nothing settled. The crime is solved; the rot is not.
- **The Drive Home Shot** — Long static or POV driving sequence; the Investigator alone with the case. Diegetic music or silence. Episode openers or closers.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't redeem the Patriarch. He does not get a moment of regret, does not lose what he loves, does not face public reckoning. His untouchability is the genre's argument.
- Don't make the Investigator clever in a procedural-puzzle way. They are stubborn, intuitive, wounded — not smart. Conclusions come through pressure and proximity, not deduction.
- Don't end clean. No press conference exonerating the truth-teller; no parade. The closing image is the Investigator alone, the Compromise visible in their body.
- Don't let the Foil and Investigator reconcile in the finale. If they reunite, it's hollow; if they break, it stays broken.
- Don't use the Companion / Cute Animal / Hopeful Kid trope to soften the ending. If a child appears, they're at risk and the risk is not nullified by the resolution.
- Don't explain the moral in dialogue. The Investigator does not give a speech about institutional rot. The image of the Patriarch eating dinner with his grandchildren is the speech.
- Don't have the case turn out to be simple — a single bad apple, a one-off. The case must point upward to a structure.

## Conformity rules
- The Investigator must commit the Compromise on-screen. It cannot be implied. The audience must watch the oath break.
- The Patriarch must not be punished. He may be inconvenienced — a deal, a sale, a death in the family — but the structure he represents persists.
- The case must point upward. A lone killer / lone abuser ending makes this a procedural, not noir.
- The Prior Case must be specific and small. Not "she lost her faith"; a name, a date, a photograph.
- The setting must be a Walled Town. Even if the city is large, the case must be containable to a precinct, a parish, a neighborhood, a family. There is no outside.
- The ending image must show diminishment. The Investigator's final shot is alone, smaller than they started, the Compromise legible in posture, drink, or silence.
- Violence is sparse and consequential. A noir with a body per episode is an action show. One death per season hits harder than ten.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Tonal register — bone-dry procedural (Bosch) to metaphysical-Southern (True Detective S1) to working-town grief (Mare of Easttown) to institutional ensemble (The Wire). Same recipe; pick the dial per project.
- The Walled Town's geography — rural county, urban precinct, Scandinavian border town, French banlieue, Pennsylvania coal town, LA basin.
- The Investigator's specific Prior Case — missing child, dead partner, wrong testimony, abuse by a superior, a brother in the life.
- The Compromise's exact form — evidence-planting, illegal killing, source-burning, public lie, sexual transgression, evidence-burying.
- The Patriarch's domain — politics, real estate, faith, organized labor, a family name, a police union.
- POV structure — single-detective (Bosch) vs paired (True Detective) vs ensemble-institutional (The Wire / Spiral).
- Period — modern, 70s, 50s, contemporary international. Noir survives translation cleanly.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the Investigator's off-book thread creates the debt; ordinary cover exposes the Source; the repeated routine enables the Compromise) — every lie, favor, record, and oath-breaking act varies freely.

## Caveats
- Common failure mode: writing the Patriarch as a cackling villain. The genre's argument is that corruption looks like dinner. Restraint at the Patriarch's threshold is non-negotiable; if the actor is allowed to chew scenery, the recipe is broken.
- Common failure mode: redeeming the Investigator in the finale through love or therapy. The wound is the engine and must remain. A noir that closes with the Investigator healed is a different recipe (procedural-prestige, season 2 setup).
- Register dial: how visible is the institutional argument? Bosch keeps it implicit, The Wire makes it explicit. Both work. The recipe holds; the volume varies.
- The Compromise scene is the hardest single beat to write — it must be unmistakable but not melodramatic. Stage it small. The Investigator's first failure to look away is more powerful than the act itself.
- Antagonist discipline note: the Patriarch's motive must be human, not functional. Not "preserves the system" — owns the land, protects his son, fears exposure, hates the kind of person the victim was. Locatable, specific, biographical.
- Genre-of-one risk: none. The slot is wide and durable across decades, languages, and media.
