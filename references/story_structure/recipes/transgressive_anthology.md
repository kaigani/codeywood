# Transgressive Sci-Fi Anthology (Memory-Leak Frame)

## Slot
Adult animated sci-fi anthology where each standalone episode renders one timeline of techno-erotic transgression collapsing under the weight of its own appetite, framed by a sadistic narrator-host who rates and devours the episode at the end. Anthology_v4 lives here.

## Corpus
- *Love, Death + Robots* (Netflix)
- *Superjail!* (Adult Swim)
- *AKIRA* (Otomo)
- *Cyberpunk: Edgerunners* (Trigger)
- *Devilman Crybaby* (Yuasa)
- *Ghost in the Shell: Innocence* (Oshii)
- *Texhnolyze* (Hamazaki)
- *Serial Experiments Lain* (Nakamura)
- *Belladonna of Sadness* (Yamamoto)
- *Genocidal Organ* (Murase)
- *Aeon Flux* (Chung)
- *Heavy Metal* (Bakshi-adjacent)
- *Junji Ito Maniac* (Netflix)
- *Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within* (Sakaguchi — for the body-horror anchor)

(Tonal range: Love Death + Robots' episodic spectacle through Edgerunners' tragic-romantic through Devilman Crybaby's apocalyptic-erotic to Belladonna's painterly transcendence. All carry the same engine.)

## Emotional contract
**Transgressive desire + visceral consequence + spectacular ruin.** The viewer pays to watch protagonists who WANT THE FORBIDDEN THING, get the forbidden thing, and pay for it on screen in beautiful, escalating, body-loud horror. The catharsis is recognition: *we begged for this; we deserve to watch ourselves consumed.*

## Cast (FFAR+C)

This is an anthology — most casts EXIST for one episode only. There are two cast layers: PROJECT-LEVEL (the frame) and EPISODE-LEVEL (the per-episode story).

### Project-level cast (the frame)

| Role | Function | Flaw | Causal obligation | Arc (from → to, turn beat) | Relational position |
|------|----------|------|-------------------|----------------------------|---------------------|
| **The Narrator-Host (e.g. Motherboard)** | Opens and closes each episode; rates the timeline 1-10 on the season's signature scale; devours the timeline on-screen at the end | Sadistic delight in audience complicity | During Calibration, the Host chooses a rating rule that rewards visible excess, then selects each Cold Open's ruin image to prime that appetite. The escalating scores invite the Recursive Episode to answer her; the Final Frame applies her own rule to her. | Doesn't change across episodes — but in the season finale, may reveal a personal stake (her own origin, her ratings being read by something larger, her own appetite turning on her) (turn: The Recursive Episode) | Outside / above every episode; the viewer's mirror. The audience IS the Narrator's audience. |

### Episode-level cast (recurs in shape, not in person)

| Role | Function | Flaw | Causal obligation | Arc (from → to, turn beat) | Relational position |
|------|----------|------|-------------------|----------------------------|---------------------|
| **The Appetite (episode protagonist)** | Wants the forbidden thing more than they want to live; the engine of the story | The appetite IS the flaw — but it grew from an ordinary human wound (loneliness, poverty, shame, grief, dysphoria, rage). No GOAL behind it — only the hunger | In Beat 3, the Appetite accepts terms whose threshold is visibly missing; after the first symptom, they choose the offered redose in Beat 6. | **The Vicious Antagonist (charismatic monster)** | Embodies the SYSTEM that produced the appetite; corporate god / pleasure cult leader / jealous AI / former human turned inside out; OFTEN the supplier of the bargain | Scale of conviction — they cannot conceive of restraint; they ARE excess incarnated | In Beat 4, the Antagonist honors the requested experience at unsafe scale instead of moderating it. | **The Witness** | The bystander / lover / hostage / coworker who has to watch the Appetite burn; the audience's POV within the episode | Complicity by observation — they could intervene and don't | Seeing the Glamour Slip in Beat 6, the Witness stays and records instead of severing the apparatus. The Appetite accepts that framing in Beat 6, feeding the transformation the System credits by name. | Amused; comments on the proceedings; sometimes credits the Appetite by name in the closing scroll (turn: Beat 5) | Above the action, but UNDER the Narrator-Host |
| **The Mirror (optional)** | A figure already further down the same transformation than the Appetite — a cyberpsycho, a previous test subject, a fully-converted cultist, an earlier discarded model. Shows the Appetite (and the audience) the endpoint | Crossed the threshold without integration — they ARE what is coming | When used, the Mirror chooses to expose its transformed body as the conduit in Beat 6. The Appetite takes that route in Beat 7, and Collapse repeats the endpoint; without a Mirror, the first symptom reveals the same bodily route. | Usually past arc already: a fixed grotesque or prophetic state the Appetite is accelerating toward (turn: Beat 6) | Glimpsed, not befriended; the Appetite says "that won't be me" and the audience knows it will |

Episode casts typically run 4-7 named roles total. The Appetite + Tempter + Antagonist + Witness is the minimum; the Mirror is the highest-value optional addition.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (the engine):**
- **The Bargain Is Honored** — Every transgressive bargain is fulfilled. The Appetite gets the thing. The cost is exacted. No backing out, no rescue, no last-minute reversal.
- **Desire as Inciting Incident** — The episode never starts with an external threat. It starts with an internal HUNGER. The world is incidental until the appetite triggers it.
- **Body as Battlefield** — Transformation happens ON the body. Augmentation, modification, fusion, fission, replication. The viewer must see the cost.
- **Beauty in Collapse** — The visual register at the moment of ruin is GLORIOUS. Spectacle is the aesthetic religion.
- **The Narrator's Rating** — Every episode closes with the Narrator-Host scoring it on the signature scale (e.g., 1-10 Orgasm-to-Apocalypse), then devouring the timeline.

**Supporting tropes (stack 3-5 per episode):**
- **Neural-Link Intimacy** — Sex, transcendence, or annihilation through cognitive interfaces.
- **Corporate Theology** — Religious devotion to a brand / product / AI.
- **The Modification Cult** — A pleasure cult, body-cult, or hyper-optimization sect that the protagonist enters.
- **Pleasure Cuts Both Ways** — The thing that gives ecstasy is the thing that kills.
- **The Charismatic Monster's Monologue** — Antagonist gets a long, articulate, almost-convincing monologue justifying the carnage.
- **The Glamour That Slips** — A perfect surface starts to fail (a glitched smile, leaking fluid, a syncopated heartbeat). Audience knows before the protagonist does.
- **The Mirror Already Crossed** — The Appetite glimpses someone further down the identical path: a cyberpsycho, a converted cultist, an earlier discarded model. The Mirror is the endpoint made visible; the Appetite's denial ("that won't be me") is the audience's certainty that it will.
- **The Witness's Final Frame** — Episode ends on the Witness's face, transformed or transfixed.
- **The City as Body** — Architecture, infrastructure, traffic, billboards all read as a vast biological organism. The City wants too.
- **The Recursive Mirror** — A character realizes they are being watched; the Narrator winks at the audience.
- **The Operatic Monologue** — At least one character gets a Shakespeare-level breakdown / declaration / curse delivered straight to camera.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- No moral lesson. The audience does not walk away with a takeaway. The takeaway IS the spectacle.
- No survivor — no clean escape. The Appetite never walks away human and intact, never learns or grows out of it. But "consumed" has modes (see Variation rules): annihilation is only one of them.
- No redemption arc. The Tempter does not repent. The Antagonist does not soften.
- No happy reunion. The Witness's scar is permanent.
- No "the system" is defeated. The City consumes; the Narrator devours; the next episode follows.
- No protagonist with a quest. The protagonist has APPETITE, not GOAL.

## Visual register

Transgression is a VISUAL contract — the concept-art phase (Step 7) draws from this section directly.

- **Color** — radioactive neon, bruised purple, surgical white, arterial red, sickly green, oily black, flesh pink, religious gold. Saturation runs hot; the palette itself should feel unsafe.
- **Texture** — chrome against skin, wet concrete, cables routed like veins, fluid and static and ash, decaying luxury, medical surfaces, viscera rendered as ornament.
- **Motion** — sudden stillness then violent eruption; bodies stretching past anatomy; looping ritual violence; hallucinated montage; transformation staged as choreography, not accident.
- **Design principle** — every object should read as becoming one of four things: flesh, machine, signal, or myth. A frame trending toward none of them is set-dressing — cut it.

## Beat spine

This is a two-layer spine: SEASON-LEVEL frame curve + PER-EPISODE story arc.

### Season-level curve (8-10 episodes)

| Position | Beat | Emotional target |
|---|---|---|
| Eps 1-3 | **Calibration** — the Host chooses a specific rating scale and rewards visible appetite, bodily cost, and Witness complicity; the audience learns the Narrator's voice and registers transgression as the show's currency. Each score remains on her display instead of disappearing. | Adjustment. Audience finds out how much they can take. |
| Eps 4-6 | **Escalation** — chasing scores above her retained baseline, the Host selects steeper bargains, more transformed bodies, and more scarred witnesses. Push past the audience's comfort; the scale begins changing what she chooses to devour. | Compulsive engagement. They look away and they can't. |
| Eps 7-8 | **Peak Transgression** — the Host chooses timelines that exceed the scale, so the season's hardest episodes leave ratings the instrument cannot contain. Audience squirms; an earlier Witness notices the overflow reaching back through consumed files. | Awe + revulsion. "This is the best of the season." |
| Ep 9 | **The Recursive Episode** — the surviving Witness follows the retained scores through the overflow and enters a new timeline carrying an earlier final frame. The Narrator references her own rating; when she chooses to score the intrusion instead of stopping it, the meta-frame cracks. | Vertigo. The show is watching the audience. |
| Ep 10 | **The Final Frame** — the recursive timeline applies the Host's accumulated scale to the appetite that selected and devoured it. The Narrator-Host's own appetite reveals itself; she is rated; the season closes on the question of who is consuming whom. | Recognition. The viewer is the next episode. |

## Conformity rules
- **Every episode ends with the Narrator-Host's rating and devouring.** No exceptions. The frame is the season's spine.
- **The bargain is honored.** No saves, no last-minute reversals, no rescue. The audience came to watch the cost.
- **Transgression must be VISUAL and CORPOREAL.** The body is the battlefield. Off-screen consequence is a failure of the form.
- **The Appetite drives the plot.** Not external threat, not a quest. Internal hunger is the engine.
- **No moral lesson.** The takeaway is the spectacle, not a teaching.
- **The Narrator-Host is sadistic, articulate, and amused.** Their voice is the season's signature.
- **Adult animated register.** Hand-drawn or stylized 3D; never live-action photorealism. The aestheticization of the body is non-negotiable.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- **Subgenre per episode** — cyberpunk / pleasure cult / corporate theology / sex virus / planetary fuck-god / body-hack underground / consciousness-upload commodification / etc. Each episode picks one.
- **Tonal register within transgression** — operatic (Devilman Crybaby), clinical (Texhnolyze), orgiastic (Belladonna), kinetic (Edgerunners), surreal (Lain).
- **Specific consequence shape** — decay, fusion, fission, replication, recursion, dissolution.
- **The mode of consumption (how the episode ends)** — beautiful annihilation (death, ruin), posthuman ascension (the Appetite becomes something past human — a god, a network, a swarm), or dissolution (they disperse into signal, data, myth). AKIRA ascends; Edgerunners annihilates; Lain dissolves. Always consumed; never cleanly escaped.
- **The Witness's relationship to the Appetite** — lover, sibling, employee, stranger, child, observer-by-coincidence.
- **The Antagonist's specific obsession** — pleasure, replication, devouring, watching, breeding, broadcasting, optimizing.
- **The signature rating scale** — Orgasm-to-Apocalypse is anthology_v4's; other instances of the recipe could use Ecstasy-to-Annihilation, Bliss-to-Bottom-Out, etc.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the named appetite accepts the bargain; the first cost prompts a chosen redose; transformation supplies Collapse and the Host's rating) — every hunger, threshold, body shape, final frame, and score varies freely.

## Caveats
- This is a HARD slot to nail because the audience tolerance band is narrow and culturally variable. Push too far in one direction = exploitation; pull back too far = prestige adjacency that loses the engine.
- **Format note — use `transgressive_serial` for pipeline runs.** The divergence pipeline produces 10-episode SERIALIZED seasons; this recipe's internal anthology structure (a host framing 8-10 standalone episodes inside one run) does not fit that. The serialized single-protagonist counterpart — `transgressive_serial` — carries the same engine in a pipeline-native shape; use it for runs. This anthology recipe is retained as the spec for a future `anthology_phase` (each logline = one standalone episode). In current practice each pipeline run is treated as one chapter of a meta-anthology that lives ACROSS runs — the anthology exists at the project level, and the host/rating frame is a per-project layer rather than a per-run one.
- **Gemma's safety training resists this slot specifically.** The brief's most transgressive pillars (sex, melodrama, operatic body horror) get progressively scrubbed at each pipeline step. The recipe enforces them as conformity rules; downstream commercial-accessibility clauses must NOT translate transgression into restraint. (See `feedback_commercial_accessibility.md` — the editor's clause needs an exception for transgressive briefs.)
- The Narrator-Host is THE marketable property. Motherboard is anthology_v4's. Without a strong host character, the season doesn't cohere.
- Watch the rating scale — it must be SPECIFIC and CONSISTENT across episodes. The audience plays along with the scale; betraying it (a 9.5 episode that wasn't actually that transgressive) breaks the contract.
