# Slasher (Fear + Pursuit + Survival)

## Slot
Serialized or single-season horror in which a KILLER with a SIGNATURE METHOD targets a DEFINED GROUP — campers, teens, sorority, family reunion, isolated tourists — and a FINAL GIRL (or her structural equivalent) survives by becoming the version of herself the group required her to be. Tension is recurring proximity to death; the killer is patient; the kills are staged; the survivor is forged. The recipe runs from grindhouse-grimy to elevated-meta with the same engine.

## Corpus
- *Halloween* (Carpenter)
- *Scream* (Craven / Williamson)
- *Friday the 13th* (Cunningham + sequels)
- *A Nightmare on Elm Street* (Craven)
- *The Texas Chain Saw Massacre* (Hooper)
- *I Know What You Did Last Summer* (Gillespie)
- *Child's Play* (Holland)
- *Final Destination* (Wong)
- *Saw* (Wan)
- *Terrifier* (Leone)
- *X / Pearl / MaXXXine* (Ti West)
- *Black Christmas* (Clark, 1974)
- *Happy Death Day* (Landon)
- *You're Next* (Wingard)
- *Hush* (Flanagan)

(Tonal range: Terrifier's grindhouse-grotesque and TCM's documentary-grim through Friday the 13th's franchise-pulp and Scream's meta-aware to Ti West's elevated-period and Happy Death Day's comic-loop. Same recipe — defined group, signature killer, the survivor who refuses the role assigned her.)

## Emotional contract
**Fear + pursuit + survival.** The audience meets the group, marks the survivor without knowing it, and then watches the killer reduce the group to her — each kill staged as both spectacle and forecast, the survivor's competence assembling under pressure. Catharsis is not justice. Catharsis is endurance — the moment she stops running and stands. The recipe is morally legible by who lives, and the genre's audience reads the morality before the credits.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Signature Method** — The killer's weapon and approach are unmistakable. Michael's knife and mask, Freddy's glove, Ghostface's robe and voice, Jason's machete, Leatherface's chainsaw and apron, the Saw mechanisms, Pearl's pitchfork. The recipe demands an aesthetic the audience can recognize in silhouette.
- **The Cold-Open Kill** — Pre-credits sequence, often featuring a character the audience does not yet know, established as a self-contained mini-story that ends in death. Establishes method, register, geography. Recipe's covenant signal.
- **The Final Girl Marker** — Early scene that codes the survivor — responsibility, grief, restraint, observation, refusal of group behavior. The audience reads it before they consciously notice it.
- **The Group Reduction** — The kills follow a sequencing that delivers the group's worst behavior first, then escalates to characters the audience has grown attached to. The recipe's emotional curve.
- **The Stand** — Late-season moment when the Final Girl stops running and engages. Often domestic — kitchen, garage, basement. She uses what is in the room. The genre's signature image.

**Supporting tropes:**
- **The False Alarm** — Early scare that turns out to be a friend, a cat, a draft. Calibrates the audience's startle reflex; recipe relies on it.
- **The Phone That Dies** — Phone runs out, gets cut, gets snatched, has no signal. Genre's signature isolation mechanism (post-2000s the recipe just upgrades the failure modes).
- **The Car That Won't Start** — Escape vehicle fails. The Place closes around the group.
- **The Death Order** — The sequencing of deaths reads the morality. Often the cruelest, drunkest, or most reckless go first. Subverted by elevated slashers, but the audience reads the inversion as commentary.
- **The Long Hallway / The Slow Walk** — Killer pursues at walking pace as the Final Girl runs / cripples / falls. The recipe's tension grammar.
- **The Mask Removal** — Late-act reveal — the mask comes off, the identity surfaces, the motive lands. Sometimes the killer is a known character; sometimes a returning figure; sometimes a stranger whose backstory frames the season.
- **The Final Girl Gets the Weapon** — She arms herself with something from the Place — kitchen knife, fireplace poker, branch, nail gun, the killer's own weapon. The transfer of method is the transfer of role.
- **The Killer Won't Die** — Killer survives stab / fall / fire / pool / bullet at least once. The recipe's resurrection beat. Sometimes literal supernatural (Michael, Freddy, Jason), sometimes just "she didn't check."
- **The Sex/Death Adjacency** — Sex scene precedes death (1978-1996 register). Modern slashers play with the trope — invert, satirize, ignore. Pattern remains genre-marker.
- **The Detective's File** — Investigator / Authority finds prior incidents, news clippings, photographs that reveal the killer's history. The recipe's worldbuilding receipt.
- **The Drunk One Gets It** — Intoxicated character dies during or shortly after intoxication. Morality embedded in the killing structure.
- **The Cabin / The House / The Camp** — The Place's signature interior. Recipe demands a constrained space where the killer can stage repeated near-misses.
- **The Stalk POV** — Camera occupying the killer's perspective for a sustained sequence. The audience put in the killer's body briefly; the recipe's complicity beat.
- **The Returning Killer** — Coda or stinger that signals the killer's possible return. Recipe's franchise gesture, but used even in standalones for ambiguity.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't show the killer's full motive in the cold open. The audience must read the killer as method first, person second. Identity / motive arrive late, often at the unmask.
- Don't kill the Final Girl. Even when the genre subverts (Drew Barrymore opening Scream), the structural Final Girl survives. Killing her is the wrong recipe.
- Don't have the group be undifferentiated. Each Doomed Friend gets one defining trait the audience can name. Without it, the deaths do not land.
- Don't resolve the killer with police arriving in time. The Final Girl must do the work. Authority is recipe-late if it arrives at all.
- Don't soften the killings. The recipe demands the kills land — staging, sound, choreography, even at PG-13 register, must register impact. Cutaway-only slashers leak.
- Don't equalize all deaths. The Death Order is the moral architecture. Even an elevated slasher reads the sequence; refusing to sequence is refusing the recipe.
- Don't let the Final Girl save the group cleanly. The recipe requires losses she could not prevent. Her survival is purchased.

## Conformity rules
- The killer must have a Signature Method visible by the cold open. Generic violence is the wrong genre.
- The Place must be specific, mappable, and constrained. Open-ended urban thrillers are not slashers.
- The Final Girl must be coded different from the group early. Not necessarily virginal — the modern recipe allows many markers — but readably set apart.
- The group must be differentiated. Each Doomed Friend has one nameable trait. Undifferentiated bodies break the recipe.
- The Death Order must be deliberate. Whether honoring or subverting the morality, the sequencing reads. Random kill order leaks.
- The Final Girl must defeat (or escape) the killer with her own action. Adult / police rescue is recipe-violation.
- The killer survives, returns, or escalates at the end — even if defeated, a stinger ambiguates. The recipe rejects clean closure.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Register — grindhouse-grimy (TCM, Terrifier), franchise-pulp (Friday the 13th, Halloween sequels), meta-aware (Scream, Cabin in the Woods adjacency), elevated-period (Ti West), comic (Happy Death Day, Tucker & Dale), home-invasion (You're Next, Hush). Same recipe runs across.
- Killer type — masked silent (Michael, Jason, Ghostface), supernatural (Freddy, Chucky), human / unmasked (Hannibal-adjacent), gameplay (Saw's Jigsaw), period (Pearl, MaXXXine). Pick the killer's species deliberately; the audience reads it.
- Group composition — teens at camp, sorority, family reunion, suburban friends, tourists abroad, film crew, deaf woman alone (Hush — group of one), trapped strangers (Saw). Recipe scales to group size.
- Final Girl's marker — virginal (1970s-80s), responsible (1990s), trauma-survivor (2000s), already-killer (You're Next, Pearl). Marker is dial; survival role is constant.
- Era — period (Pearl, 1978's Halloween), contemporary (most), post-meta (Scream, Cabin), supernatural-period mix (Nightmare on Elm Street, Sleepaway Camp). Era is register dial.
- The Investigator / Knower's role — peer (Randy in Scream), authority figure who converts, no Knower at all (TCM, Halloween 1978). Optional but useful.
- Ending — escape (Hush), kill (Halloween), trapped-with-killer (TCM), ambiguous return (most), loop (Happy Death Day). All valid; choose for the recipe's last verdict.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the dismissal exposes a friend; the trap teaches the Place; the wrong suspect isolates the survivor; the Stand consumes her self-trust and the Place-map) — the CONTENT of every edge varies freely.

## Caveats
- Failure mode: undifferentiated group. If the audience can't name each Doomed Friend's trait, the deaths don't land. Specificity, not population, is the recipe's group rule. (Now encoded in the wiring: E2-group-bonds and E8's consumed choices.)
- Failure mode: the Final Girl as cipher. She must be marked specifically — what makes her survive must be a trait the audience saw in Episode 1, not a power-up acquired later. (Now encoded in the wiring: E1-final-girl-marker, E7-self-trust, E10.)
- Failure mode: cutting away from the kills. Even at PG-13 the recipe demands impact registers — sound, framing, aftermath. Sanitized kills break the engine.
- Failure mode: the killer over-explained. Motive arrives late and partial. Reveal in Ep 3 and the rest is post-script. (Now encoded in the wiring: E6-incomplete-wound delays E9's reveal.)
- Failure mode: police save the day. Adults are recipe-late. If the Skeptic / Authority resolves the threat, the Final Girl never gets her Stand. (Now encoded in the wiring: E8-authority-absence constrains E10.)
- Register dial: elevated slashers (Ti West, Hush, You're Next) often invert the moral grammar — the punished traits are different, the Final Girl is already a killer, the group is differently composed. The recipe survives the inversion if the structural beats hold.
- Genre proximity: home-invasion thriller (Strangers, Funny Games) sits next door — same constrained geography, but the killer is usually plural, motive is opacity-as-statement, and the group is smaller (often a single household). If the killer has no Signature Method and motive is absence, drift to home-invasion thriller.
- Genre proximity: serial-killer procedural (Hannibal, Mindhunter) is the killer-as-protagonist or killer-as-puzzle from outside. Slasher is the killer at killing time, from the victim group's POV. The POV is the line.
