# Romantasy

## Slot
Fantasy-romance hybrid where a low-status woman with awakening forbidden magic falls for a dark, powerful, near-immortal male whose monstrousness conceals devotion. Commercial neighborhood: ACOTAR / Fourth Wing / From Blood and Ash / Crescent City / The Bridge Kingdom shelf.

## Emotional contract
**Desire + danger + transformation.** The reader pays for the slow tension between a woman who can't afford to want, and the man whose protection costs her her autonomy — through to the moment the bond becomes inevitable and the world bends around it.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone — every romantasy bestseller uses one or both):**
- **Enemies to Lovers** — Two characters with genuine ideological reasons to hate each other, trapped in proximity by magic, until hate turns. Internal resistance is everything; the war inside her head is what the reader is paying for. The magic system is often the metaphor (fire + ice, shadow + light combining unexpectedly).
- **Fated Mates** — Cosmic bond / soul-deep recognition. The universe says yes; everything else says no. Resistance from both sides (autonomy / vulnerability). Built into the magic system: amplifies their power, telepathy, physical ache when apart, severe cost if rejected. Use false starts before full snap-into-place.

**Supporting tropes (stack 5-6 per book on top of the core):**
- **Touch Her and Die** — Male lead's protective fury manifests in the magic system. Environment reacts to his rage. Only works when balanced with vulnerability — the fear of losing her is what makes it romantic, not possessive.
- **Found Family** — Outcasts bonded by shared exile, curses, persecution. Choose each other over blood obligation. Raises stakes when threatened.
- **Morally Gray Hero** — Shares protagonist's goals but uses brutal methods. Romance does NOT fix him into a perfect hero — torn between ruthless instincts and growing loyalty.
- **Forced Proximity** — Magical bindings, enchanted realms, shared quarters. Give them agency within the confinement.
- **The Chosen One** — Destined to wield unique power. Anchor in magic system; use quests to build intimacy.
- **Betrayal & Plot Twists** — Trusted ally hides loyalty. Foreshadowing hidden inside the magic system. Drop the hammer around 70%.
- **Forbidden Love** — Prohibition absolute, tied to world's rules. Every stolen touch risks treason or magical backlash.
- **Slow Burn** — Subtle clues, lingering glances, accidental magical links. Micro-advances then pull-back via external threats.
- **The Deadly Trial** — Tournament where failure means death. Multi-stage, escalating. Love Interest must watch protagonist bleed.
- **Hidden Identity** — Character conceals royal blood, forbidden magic, dangerous allegiance. Hiding is costly (glamour drains life force).
- **Deal With the Devil** — Backed into a corner, pact with a powerful entity, often the love interest. Blood oath enforces; cost escalates.
- **The Gilded Cage** — Confinement in luxury, not dungeon. Captor is morally gray love interest who believes he's protecting her.
- **Age Gap (Centuries)** — Tired immortal + human who won't stop pushing. Stakes in magic (ritual to share immortality, curse that slows aging).
- **Resurrection / Second Chances** — Death as pivot, not ending. Cost is severe (life force, soul corruption). Aftermath resets the romance — return with amnesia, falling in love all over again.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't have the protagonist "fix" the Shadow Lord into a kind hero — his cruelty is performance, not a flaw to be cured.
- Don't have the Antagonist surrender or repent — they escalate to the end.
- Don't resolve the hate→love turn quickly — the war inside the protagonist's head is the engine.
- Don't have the prohibition (forbidden-love) be casual social disapproval — it must be absolute, tied to world rules.
- Don't have the Betrayer be obviously evil from page one — they must read as safe harbor, then betray.
- Don't let the Catalyst Dependent be purely passive — they reveal hidden agency or corruption midway.

## Conformity rules
- Protagonist is a low-status woman with awakening forbidden magic. Not a princess, not a Chosen One from page one.
- Love Interest is dark, feared, near-immortal, with PERFORMED cruelty masking centuries-old conviction.
- Antagonist suppresses the kind of magic the protagonist wields — the protagonist's identity IS the political threat.
- Touch Her And Die must be set up and paid off — the Love Interest's protective fury must manifest in the magic system at least once.
- The hate-to-love turn must be SLOW. Grudging respect → reluctant attraction → denial → vulnerability → inevitability. No casual flips.
- A Found Family must form. Not optional — the dark world needs the flashlight.
- The Betrayer must be set up as safe harbor before the turn. Their betrayal must register as a personal wound to the reader.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- World mechanics (magic system rules, what's forbidden, who can wield what) — invent every time.
- The "trial" / "tournament" / "ritual" container — bonded by magic, military academy, royal court, blood ceremony, gladiatorial trial.
- Tonal register — high-court romance (ACOTAR), military pulp (Fourth Wing), urban-fantasy mosaic (Crescent City), epistolary intimacy (Bridge Kingdom).
- The Catalyst Dependent's hidden agency reveal — corruption, willing cooperation, secret power.
- The midstory secret about the Love Interest — his curse, his prior love, his hidden mission, his real allegiance.
- Whether the Love Interest's secrecy is justified or villainous — varies; the conformity is that it MUST detonate.
