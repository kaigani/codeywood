# Urban Fantasy (Disorientation + Enchantment + Belonging)

## Slot
Serialized fiction set in a contemporary city (or recognizable modern world) where a mostly-ordinary protagonist crosses a MEMBRANE between mundane life and a hidden magical community — vampire courts, fae councils, witches' circles, demon underworld, deity diaspora, monster-hunter lineages, wizard guilds — and must claim a place inside both. Belonging is the recipe's payoff: earned through claimed power AND chosen allies. The city is real; the magic is real; the seams between them are where the story lives.

(Tonal range: from Buffy / Teen Wolf's pulp-YA-banter and Supernatural / Wynonna's monster-procedural through Dresden / Rivers of London's noir-investigative to Lucifer / True Blood's CW-camp-melodrama and American Gods / Sandman's prestige-mythic-poetic. Same recipe — mundane protagonist crosses the membrane, claims power, finds the new family.)

## Emotional contract
**Disorientation + enchantment + belonging.** Audience opens with the protagonist in the mundane — apartment, job, family obligation — and gets pulled across the membrane into the hidden city. Disorientation is the first dose: the rules don't work the way they were taught. Enchantment is the steady drug: the magical community is seductive, strange, sometimes brutal, always SPECIFIC. Belonging is the cocktail's payoff: the protagonist earns a place inside the hidden world while keeping (or losing) their tether to the mundane. The cocktail is the city's secret life made personal.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Membrane Crossing** — Pilot's central event: the moment the Crosser sees what cannot be unseen — vampire bite, fae glamour drops, magical wound, family secret confirmed, deity arrives, body refuses to die. The membrane was always there; now the protagonist is across it.
- **The Hidden Map** — The city has a parallel map — fae court behind the door at Ealing Broadway, vampire bar in the warehouse district, witches' coven in the back of the herb shop, the river spirits at the canal, the Sandman's library. Worldbuilding is geographical: the magical world overlays the mundane one in specific rooms.
- **The Rules That Bind** — The magical world has rules — iron burns fae, vampires need invitation, names have power, debts must be paid, salt circles hold, true names compel. The Crosser learns by violating them. Rules are the genre's procedural spine.
- **The Claimed Power** — The Crosser's specific magical capability — vampire-slaying strength, wizard's craft, deity bloodline, summoned-name authority, monster-tracker instinct, hellsong, river-listening. Manifests gradually, costs to use, is THEIRS by the climax.
- **The Found Family Across the Membrane** — By midpoint, the Crosser has assembled a small group — Sponsor + Sidekick + sometimes the Forbidden + sometimes the Mundane Anchor pulled across — that operates as their belonging.

**Supporting tropes:**
- **The Magical Bar / Safe House** — A neutral-ground venue where factions drink, talk, trade — Caritas in Lucifer, the Hellmouth's coffee shops, Fangtasia, Murphy's bar in Dresden's Chicago, the Folly in Rivers of London. Genre's diplomatic stage.
- **The Glamour / Mask** — Magical beings pass as human through illusion; the Crosser learns to see through. Used for reveals and small intimacies.
- **The Bargain / Debt** — A magical exchange the Crosser enters that has long-tail consequences — fae bargain, soul price, vampire pact, blood debt, named favor. Genre's source of recurring trouble.
- **The Lineage Reveal** — The Crosser learns their family was already in this world — grandmother was a witch, father is a demon, mother's name was a code, sibling is half-fae. Earned mid-season.
- **The Patrol / Hunt** — Episode-level procedural — a monster of the week, a missing person, a magical crime — that the team handles. Genre's pulse and case-format.
- **The Wrong Help** — Crosser tries to use mundane channels (cops, hospitals, family) on a magical problem. It backfires or gets people hurt. Lesson: the membrane has rules.
- **The Court Summons** — Crosser is brought before the Council / Court / Coven to explain themselves. Audience meets the politics of the hidden world. Often the season's act-2 turn.
- **The True Name** — Identities have magical weight — birth name, hidden name, secret name, demon-name; speaking them compels or binds. Frequent climax leverage.
- **The Ritual Scene** — A formal magical procedure — summoning, banishing, sealing, hand-fasting, healing, oath-taking — rendered as theatrical and SPECIFIC. Camera-friendly set piece.
- **The Cop / Detective / Friend Who Doesn't Believe** — A mundane authority figure who keeps almost catching the truth; sometimes converts (becomes Sidekick) or stays a managed obstacle.
- **The Underworld Tourism Hour** — One episode mid-season where the Crosser goes deeper into the hidden city — demon nightclub, fae court, ghost market, vampire mansion, witches' sabbat. The genre's pleasure-dose.
- **The Forbidden Kiss / First Touch** — Romantic / sexual moment between the Crosser and the Forbidden that confirms the danger; usually act 2 out or act 3 in.
- **The Cost of Magic** — Every spell, every blood-drink, every fae bargain, every demon summon owes — fatigue, blood, time, memory, allegiance. The genre's body-tally.
- **The Old Enemy Returns** — Mid-late season, a prior season's antagonist surfaces — old vampire master, escaped demon, banished fae lord — as background pressure or ally-of-convenience.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't have the Crosser already inside the magical at episode 1. The recipe is the CROSSING — the disorientation is the engine. Pre-crossed protagonists belong to high fantasy or paranormal romance.
- Don't let the Mundane Anchor never matter. If the protagonist's tether to the mundane is ignored, the cost of crossing evaporates and the recipe collapses to fantasy.
- Don't make the magical world a costume on a cop show. The Hidden Map must have real institutions, factions, rules — politics that work even when the protagonist isn't looking.
- Don't reveal everything in dialogue. The Hidden Map and the Rules That Bind are LEARNED by the Crosser through experience — bargain made, glamour dropped, debt called, name spoken. Worldbuilding through behavior.
- Don't make the Forbidden a flat alpha. The romantic / magical temptation must have its own life, ethics, history — Spike has a soul-curse arc; Bill Compton has Civil War trauma; the Endless are not boyfriends. Forbidden must read as a person, not a posture.
- Don't make the Court / Council faceless. The institution must have NAMED human-or-magical members who speak, vote, bicker, betray. If the Council is "the elders" with no faces, the politics evaporate.
- Don't kill the Mundane Anchor in episode 2. The cost of crossing is felt across the season — protect the tether at least to midpoint so its eventual breach or accommodation lands.

## Conformity rules
- The Crosser must START IN THE MUNDANE. The recipe's engine is the disorientation of crossing. Pre-crossed protagonists belong to high fantasy or paranormal romance.
- The membrane between mundane and magical must be SPECIFIC and geographical — named bars, named courts, named buildings, named rivers. The Hidden Map must be drawable.
- The Rules That Bind must be learned through behavior and cost, not exposition. Iron burns fae, names compel, salt holds — and the Crosser learns by breaking them.
- The Mundane Anchor must exist on screen and be specifically named. Without them the cost of crossing evaporates and the recipe slides to fantasy.
- The Claimed Power must manifest gradually and cost. By the climax, the Crosser uses a magical capability that is THEIRS — earned, costly, characteristic.
- The Found Family must form across the membrane by midpoint. Belonging is mutual; one body's claim alone is not enough.
- The Court / Council must have faces — named members who speak, vote, betray. Faceless institutions evaporate the genre's politics.
- The Forbidden must be a person with their own life, not a posture. Romantic / magical temptation must read as compatible — not just hot.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Tonal register — pulp-YA-banter (Buffy, Teen Wolf, Shadowhunters), noir-investigative (Dresden, Rivers of London, Sandman's detective beats), camp-melodrama (Lucifer, True Blood, Wynonna Earp), prestige-mythic (American Gods, Sandman, Magicians late), procedural-monster (Supernatural, Wynonna, Buffy at episode level).
- The hidden world's substrate — vampire / supernatural (Buffy, True Blood, Vampire Diaries), wizard / craft (Dresden, Magicians, Rivers of London), fae / Sidhe (October Daye, Dresden's later books, Sandman's Faerie), deity (American Gods, Lucifer, Sandman), monster-hunter lineage (Supernatural, Shadowhunters, Wynonna), demon / underworld (Lucifer, Constantine, Good Omens).
- The Crosser's mundane substrate — student (Buffy), cop / detective (Dresden, Rivers of London, Wynonna), bartender (Sookie), librarian (Buffy's Giles-adjacent), journalist, professional, parent, sibling, gravedigger (October Daye). Each register dial.
- City — generic-Sunnydale (Buffy, Teen Wolf), Chicago (Dresden), London (Rivers of London, Sandman), New York (Shadowhunters, Sandman), LA (Lucifer, True Blood-adjacent), Tulsa / Purgatory (Wynonna). Each register dial.
- Court structure — formal-aristocratic (fae, vampire courts), guild-bureaucratic (wizards' Council, monster-hunter Conclave), pantheon-political (deity diaspora in American Gods), informal-coven (witches), corporate (Lucifer's later structures).
- Whether the Forbidden becomes partner, sacrifice, or recurring shadow — register dial. Buffy-Angel-Spike spans all three. All conform.
- Whether the Mundane Anchor is pulled across or kept apart — register dial. Sookie's mundane brother gets pulled in slowly; Dresden's brother is already across; Buffy's mom learns mid-series. All conform.
- How explicit the city is — supernatural-everywhere (True Blood after the reveal) vs strictly-hidden (Dresden's secrecy doctrine). Register dial.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the Cost consumes a planted lie and a burned standing; the Summons consumes the breach; the climax consumes the name, the settled bargain, and the kiss) — the CONTENT of every edge varies freely: which substrate, whose debt opens the underworld, what the surge owes, what the tether finally pays.

## Caveats
- Common failure mode: pre-crossed protagonist. If the Crosser is already a wizard / hunter / vampire at episode 1 with no disorientation, the recipe collapses to fantasy or paranormal procedural. The CROSSING is the show's engine.
- Common failure mode: the magical as costume. If the show could be played as a normal cop show with monsters swapped for criminals, the Hidden Map has failed. The hidden world's politics, institutions, and stakes must operate independently of the cases.
- Common failure mode: mundane tether dropped. If the protagonist's mundane life vanishes by Episode 3 — no friend, no sibling, no job, no parent — the cost of crossing evaporates. Buffy's mom and Dawn matter; Dresden's brother matters; Sookie's Bon Temps matters. (Now encoded in the wiring: E1-kept-secret → E4-anchor-lie → E8-torn-lie → E10's renegotiated tether.)
- Common failure mode: the Forbidden as posture. A dangerous lover who is just dark eyeliner and brooding reads as paranormal-romance-by-the-numbers. Spike and Bill and Lucifer have ARCS, ethics, complications. Compatibility must be earned. (The wiring now gives them decisions to own: E5-forbidden-warning, E8-faction-betrayal.)
- Register dial: belonging tone — the show can lean toward earned-by-choice (Buffy's Scooby Gang, Dresden's circle) or earned-by-blood (Shadowhunters' lineage, Wynonna's Earp curse). Both work; the former scales tonally lighter, the latter tonally heavier.
- Register dial: case-of-the-week density. Procedural-heavy shows (Supernatural, Buffy early, Dresden, Wynonna) run patrol / monster-of-the-week as primary spine with arc trickled in; arc-heavy shows (Sandman, Magicians late, American Gods) sublimate the case beats into the larger mystery. Recipe accommodates both; declare register at intake.
- Genre proximity: paranormal romance has the same membrane but the love story is the engine; urban fantasy has the SHOW (case, faction, lineage) as the engine with romance as a thread. If the central question is "will they end up together," the slot is paranormal romance, not urban fantasy.
