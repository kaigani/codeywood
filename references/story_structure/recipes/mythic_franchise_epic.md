# Mythic Franchise Epic

## Slot
Tentpole serialized or feature storytelling where capital-G Gods, primal beings, or hyper-powered factions wage war across cosmic stakes, and a mortal-or-mortal-adjacent protagonist becomes part of myth by the ending. Unlike epic fantasy quest (one journey, one destiny), the mythic epic is a pantheon — multiple powers, dynastic time, succession. The protagonist either ascends, dies into legend, or survives to be the next age's god.

## Corpus
- *Marvel's Avengers / Infinity War / Endgame* (MCU)
- *Eternals* (Marvel)
- *Thor: Ragnarok* (Marvel)
- *Wonder Woman* (DC)
- *Percy Jackson* (Riordan / Disney+)
- *Clash of the Titans* / *Wrath of the Titans*
- *American Gods* (Gaiman / Starz)
- *The Sandman* (Gaiman / Netflix)
- *God of War* (Kratos cycle, Santa Monica Studio)
- *The Northman* (Eggers)
- *Star Wars (original trilogy + Force-mythic side)*
- *Dune* (Villeneuve)
- *Foundation* (Asimov / Apple)
- *House of the Dragon* / *Game of Thrones* (Targaryen / Children of the Forest tier)
- *Harry Potter* (lean — Deathly Hallows mythic register)

(Tonal range: camp-Hercules through reverent-Wonder Woman through bleak-Northman to austere-Dune. Same engine — pantheon, war, apotheosis — dialed from popcorn to ritual.)

## Emotional contract
**Pantheon + war + apotheosis.** The audience feels the awe of beings larger than themselves, the dread of a war whose stakes are cosmic, and the catharsis of watching a mortal (or near-mortal) become part of myth — either ascending, dying into legend, or surviving as the seed of the next age. The cocktail is reverence + scale + transformation-into-story.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (the engine):**
- **The Pantheon Reveal.** The first or second act unveils the full scale of the powers in play — a council, a vision, a war room of gods. Awe-beat. The audience learns the chessboard.
- **The Mortal-Touched Bloodline.** The protagonist is part-god, prophesied, or marked. The signature is visible — a scar, an eye, an emblem, a power that surfaces under pressure.
- **The Cosmic War.** The conflict is not local. Worlds, ages, or realities are at stake. Battles happen at the scale of mountains, planets, or dreams.
- **The Apotheosis.** The protagonist's final transformation — they become a god, die into myth, or birth the next age. The cost is mortality, anonymity, or both.
- **The Fall of the Tyrant.** The ranking god falls — to the Heir, to time, to their own pride. The fall is the season's climax.

**Supporting tropes (stack 5-8 per work):**
- **The Sacred Object.** A relic that gates the war — the Hammer, the Lasso, the Holy Grail, the Spice, the Infinity Stones, the Crown.
- **The Prophecy.** Half-stated, partially misunderstood; pays off at the end via reread, not reveal.
- **The Trickster's Bargain.** A deal that seems to favor one side; reveals the Trickster's actual hand at the climax.
- **The War Council.** A scene where the gods (or hyper-powered factions) argue strategy — speeches, factions, lines drawn.
- **The Descent / Underworld Journey.** The Heir must enter the realm of the dead, the place outside time, the hidden god's prison.
- **The Sacred Wound.** The Heir is marked permanently — a scar, a missing eye, a hand, an arm. Visible loss is the genre's grammar.
- **The Sibling Duel.** Equal-tier confrontation with the Warrior Sibling. Often ends in reconciliation or the sibling's death.
- **The Mortal's Death.** The Mortal Witness dies (or is permanently parted from the Heir). The cost of ascension.
- **The Chorus Splits.** The orchestrating body fragments — Eternals divide, Bene Gesserit faction-out, gods take sides.
- **The Old Age Ends.** A line of dialogue, a setting-sun shot, a coronation — the audience is told the previous age is finished.
- **The Easter Egg / Crossover.** A figure from another myth-cycle appears in cameo, signaling that the pantheon is wider than this story. Franchise-grammar.
- **The Hidden God.** A power kept secret for most of the work; revealed in the final third (the true Big Bad, the dreaming god, the secret heir).

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't make the gods small. Even camp gods (Percy Jackson Olympians) must read as bigger than humans in scale, history, and consequence.
- Don't have the Heir refuse apotheosis with no cost. The choice to remain mortal must cost something equivalent to the ascension's cost.
- Don't resolve a cosmic war with one fight scene devoid of consequence. The cosmic war must reshape the world's fundamental rules.
- Don't have the Tyrant God be a flat villain. Even the worst (Hela, Thanos in his terrible logic, Zeus the patriarch) believe themselves justified by cosmic principle.
- Don't have the Heir's mortal life return to status quo. Apotheosis or its refusal makes return impossible.
- Don't explain the pantheon's metaphysics fully. The mythic register requires withheld depth — the audience knows there's more than they're told.
- Don't end on a single triumphant note. The fall of the old age requires elegiac counterpoint.

## Conformity rules
- The pantheon must be visible. A named set of powers (not necessarily a literal Olympian council, but a clear who's-who of cosmic actors). Single-villain stories without a wider pantheon are a different recipe.
- The Tyrant God's fall must be a fall, not just a defeat. An age ends with him. A throne, a line, a worship-system breaks.
- The Heir must be visibly transformed by the ending — body, status, name, or existence. Status-quo restoration violates apotheosis.
- The Mortal Witness must pay. Their loss (death, separation, mortality outstripping the Heir) is the genre's emotional anchor.
- Scale must be cosmic. Battles, stakes, and consequences extend beyond local geography — worlds, ages, realities are at risk.
- The Sacred Object must be specific and namable. Hammer, Lasso, Stones, Crown, Spice. Generic "weapon" is undercooked.
- Western audiences accept the genre's reverent register IF the camp register is balanced against weight (Thor: Ragnarok is camp + grief). Pure camp without elegiac counterpoint is parody.
- The ending must include the old-age-ends signal. A coronation, a sunset, a line of dialogue, a memorial — the audience needs the formal sign-off.
- Project-specific note: the recipe is FLOOR. Persona-driven polemic colors the pantheon's politics (whose succession, what kind of order falls, which god is examined) but cannot remove the pantheon, the cosmic war, or the apotheosis.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Pantheon flavor — Greco-Roman (Percy, Clash, Wonder Woman), Norse (Thor, Northman, American Gods partially), modern-cosmic (MCU Eternals, Foundation, Dune), invented-mythic (Star Wars Force-mythic side, House of the Dragon, Harry Potter Hallows). Cultural mapping is wide.
- The Heir's call — birth (demigod), oath (Jedi), mark (Wonder Woman), accident (Percy's discovery), prophecy (Paul Atreides). Each generates different texture.
- Tonal register — camp (Percy, Clash), reverent (Wonder Woman, Dune), brutal (Northman, Game of Thrones mythic-tier), elegiac (Sandman, American Gods), popcorn-epic (MCU mainline).
- The Tyrant God's specific signature — patriarchal order (Zeus, Odin), conquering will (Thanos, Sauron-myth-tier), grief-twisted (Hela, the Witch-King), institutional (Lazarus / the Emperor in Dune). Pick the texture of the old age.
- Scale of the cosmic war — multiverse (MCU late), single pantheon (Percy, Wonder Woman), cyclical-age (Dune, Foundation), apocalyptic-bounded (Northman, Harry Potter mythic-tier).
- Whether the Heir survives apotheosis — survives ascended (Diana, Thor), dies into legend (Tony Stark, Frodo's diminished return), survives as seed (Paul, Harry). All three are canonical.
- The Trickster's specific habits — bargainer (Loki), revealer (Hermes), thief (Anansi), prankster-tutor (Mr. Wednesday). The Trickster is mandatory; the flavor varies.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (public naming triggers the relic war; the bargain opens the underworld but charges a blood debt; the climax consumes the scar, the schism, the prison-break, and the mortal loss) — the CONTENT of every edge varies freely: which relic, whose pride, what older imprisonment, what apotheosis costs.

## Caveats
- The genre tolerates very wide register variation. The MCU and The Northman share the recipe; they could not share a screening room.
- The franchise-grammar (Easter eggs, crossovers, world-expansion hooks) is genre-canonical. Standalone mythic epics CAN work but must compensate with extra-dense pantheon work in-text.
- This recipe overlaps with Epic Fantasy Quest at the edges. Distinguish: Epic Fantasy Quest centers on ONE journey toward ONE destination. Mythic Franchise Epic centers on a PANTHEON whose succession is the plot. If the work is about a fellowship crossing a map, use epic_fantasy_quest. If it's about gods and ages, use this recipe.
- Common failure mode: making the pantheon background flavor instead of the engine. If the gods are decorative, this is a different genre. The pantheon must MATTER — its politics, its succession, its fractures drive the plot.
- Common failure mode: apotheosis without cost. The Heir's transformation must be paid for visibly — Mortal Witness death, Sacred Wound, exile, lost humanity.
- Common failure mode: cosmic stakes that don't read cosmic on screen. Saying "the universe is at stake" without showing the universe shaped by the conflict feels hollow.
