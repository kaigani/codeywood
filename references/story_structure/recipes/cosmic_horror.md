# Cosmic Horror (Investigation + Revelation + Dissolution)

## Slot
Serialized horror where a working investigator (cop, detective, journalist, priest, scholar, parent) chases an apparent crime or anomaly into a metaphysical revelation that erases meaning. The plot is a case; the genre is what the case costs the protagonist's worldview. The audience pays in dread that compounds rather than resolves.

## Corpus
- *True Detective* S1 (HBO / Pizzolatto)
- *The Terror* S1 (AMC)
- *Annihilation* (Garland)
- *Midnight Mass* (Flanagan)
- *Lovecraft Country* (HBO / Green)
- *The Mist* (Darabont)
- *In the Mouth of Madness* (Carpenter)
- *Color Out of Space* (Stanley)
- *Event Horizon* (Anderson)
- *The Endless* (Benson/Moorhead)
- *Pontypool* (McDonald)
- *From* (MGM+)
- *Servant* (Shyamalan)
- *The Outsider* (King / HBO)

(Tonal range: True Detective's prestige-philosophical and The Terror's slow-prestige through Midnight Mass's elegiac to The Mist's pulp-grindhouse and Event Horizon's schlock-extreme. Same recipe — investigator chasing a real case into an unreal answer.)

## Emotional contract
**Dread + revelation + dissolution.** The audience starts with a procedural hook they think they understand, gets pulled deeper into anomaly, and ends with a revelation that doesn't satisfy — it unmakes the protagonist. The cocktail is the SLOW erosion of certainty. Comfort is not on the menu.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Procedural Onramp** — Pilot opens as a recognizable case: murder, missing person, ritual scene, anomaly report. The genre disguises itself as a known thing. The audience must think they're watching the case-thing for an hour before the geometry starts wrong.
- **The First Wrongness** — A small detail that doesn't fit any natural explanation — a symbol, a sound, a body in a posture the body shouldn't make. The Investigator notes and tables it. The audience knows.
- **The Hidden Layer Beneath the Town** — Every location has an under-history (a cult, a contract, a buried site, a lineage). The Threshold Figure is its keeper.
- **The Revelation That Doesn't Help** — The protagonist learns the truth and the truth doesn't equip them to act. Knowing makes them less capable, not more. The opposite of detective satisfaction.
- **The Dissolution Ending** — The protagonist ends diminished — missing a piece (memory, partner, faith, sanity, body). The audience pays in the ache of what was lost to learn.

**Supporting tropes:**
- **The Mystic's Warning** — Marginal figure (priest, addict, scholar) tells the Investigator to stop. The Investigator dismisses them. They die or break by midpoint.
- **The Recurring Symbol** — A shape, phrase, or pattern recurs across unrelated scenes (the spiral, "carcosa," the antlers). Object-permanence horror — the symbol is real.
- **The Compromised Authority** — The local institution (church, sheriff, hospital, family) is complicit. The Investigator can't call for backup; the backup is part of it.
- **The Contaminated Witness** — A character who survives an encounter is changed — speaks differently, eats wrong, watches the protagonist too long. Quiet horror.
- **The Found Document** — Journal, recording, letter, tape, footage from a prior investigator who got further than the protagonist will. Usually ends mid-sentence.
- **The Wrong Sky** — The world's natural elements behave wrong — weather, animals, light, tides. Cosmic intrusion expressed through environment rather than monster.
- **The Vessel's Lucidity** — The possessed / infected / chosen character has one clear moment — speaks normally, asks for help — before going back under. The audience's deepest cut.
- **The Partner's Last Joke** — Mid-season, the Partner cracks a routine joke that lands like grief. Marks the boundary between procedural and tragedy.
- **The Believer's Library** — The Mystic has a workspace — wall of clippings, books, maps, photographs. The Investigator visits it and starts to convert.
- **The Sacrament** — A ritual the Threshold Figure has been performing for years (Mass, sealing, marking, feeding) that the Investigator is asked to either join or interrupt.
- **The Doorway Already Open** — Midpoint reveal — the cosmic intrusion is not coming; it has been here, and the protagonist has been inside it the whole time.
- **The Language Goes** — Late-stage character can't speak right anymore (Pontypool). Loss of words is loss of self.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't let the Investigator solve it. The case may end (arrest made, body buried, town sealed) but the truth remains active. Closure is procedural; the cosmic doesn't close.
- Don't make the Cosmic explainable through dialogue. It can be shown (geometry, behavior, symptom) but never reduced to a Wikipedia paragraph. The moment it's explained, it stops being cosmic.
- Don't kill the Investigator in the climax. Survival is the punishment. They live to carry what they saw.
- Don't let the Mystic be vindicated in time to save the Investigator. The Mystic dies, breaks, or vanishes BEFORE their warning could have helped.
- Don't fight the Cosmic with weapons that work. Bullets, fire, ritual, prayer — they may delay, never resolve. If something works it's the wrong genre (that's monster horror).
- Don't reset the world. The Investigator's town / family / agency is permanently marked, even if the surface looks normal. The audience must see the seam.

## Conformity rules
- The protagonist must be a working investigator at the start — they have a job, a method, a partner, a case. The genre is the erosion of professional competence.
- The Cosmic must be SHOWN, not explained. Geometry, behavior, symptom, environment — never a Wikipedia exposition.
- The Mystic dies, breaks, or dissolves BEFORE their warning becomes actionable. Their vindication is too late.
- The Investigator survives the climax. Dissolution requires a witness who carries it forward.
- The Loved One must exist on screen — the cost of cosmic horror is domestic, specific, named. Without them the dissolution is abstract.
- A Recurring Symbol must appear in at least three unrelated scenes across the season — one of them in the final episode, in a domestic / mundane location.
- Knowing must HURT. Any beat where the protagonist learns and gains capability is the wrong genre.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Setting — vertical city, isolated town, ship, expedition, suburban home, island, ice. Cosmic intrusion is location-agnostic.
- Procedural shell — murder case, missing person, journalism, ghost story, archaeology, medical mystery, religious assignment, family inheritance. The shell determines tonal register more than the cosmic itself does.
- The Cosmic's expression — entity, weather, language, geometry, dream, contagion, time. Each register dial.
- Whether the Investigator believes by midpoint or by end. Slow-prestige drags belief to Ep 9; pulp-schlock converts by Ep 4.
- Whether the protagonist seals the truth or exposes it. Both are valid; the dissolution is identical.
- Religious vs secular framing — Midnight Mass is religious-elegiac; True Detective is philosophical-secular. Same beat spine.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (method opens the hidden layer; denial removes proof; silence routes the case into the sacrament; domestic absence makes dissolution specific) — every case, symptom, ritual, and loss varies freely.

## Caveats
- Failure mode: making the Cosmic too literal. The moment the entity has a name, a back-history, and a stated motive, the genre slides into monster horror or dark fantasy. Keep the Cosmic indifferent and partial.
- Failure mode: clean closure. Detective TV trains audiences to expect resolution. Resist. The case can close while the truth stays open — that is the genre's central craft.
- Failure mode: the Mystic as wise sage. The Mystic must be COMPROMISED — drunk, mad, marginal, dying. If they're a credentialed expert with a TED talk, the dread evaporates.
- Register dial: prestige slows the procedural onramp to two episodes; pulp jumps the First Wrongness inside Ep 1's cold open. Both work — choose deliberately.
- Genre proximity: monster horror has a creature the protagonist can fight; cosmic horror has a condition they cannot. The line is whether the antagonist has a body that can be hit.
