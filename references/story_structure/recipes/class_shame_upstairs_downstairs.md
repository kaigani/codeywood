# Class Shame (Upstairs / Downstairs)

## Slot
Drama and thriller built on a single HOUSEHOLD OR INSTITUTION that contains TWO SOCIAL CLASSES sharing space across a permeable but policed MEMBRANE — masters and servants, owners and staff, hosts and help, families and the people who serve them. The transgression of that membrane (sex, knowledge, theft, friendship, violence) is the engine; the consequence — shame, exposure, ruin, return to position — is the payoff.

## Corpus
- *Downton Abbey* (ITV) — Edwardian estate, warm-classical register
- *Gosford Park* (Altman) — country-house weekend, ironic-prestige
- *The Remains of the Day* (Ivory) — repression-prestige, literary
- *Saltburn* (Fennell) — contemporary, grotesque-satirical
- *Parasite* (Bong) — contemporary Korean, thriller-violent
- *The Help* (Taylor) — civil-rights-era US, mainstream-melodramatic
- *Belgravia* (Fellowes) — 19th-century, soft-prestige
- *Mrs Wilson* (BBC) — 20th-century, intimate-mystery
- *The Crown* — Royal household, servants and aides as supporting class membrane
- *Lady Macbeth* (Oldroyd) — 19th-century rural England, austere-violent
- *Atonement* (Wright / McEwan) — interwar England, prestige-tragic
- *The Servant* (Losey / Pinter) — psychological-erotic, 60s London
- *Rules of the Game* (Renoir) — pre-war French country house, foundational
- *The Menu* (Mylod) — contemporary restaurant, satirical-horror
- *You* (lean, season 1) — bookstore manager / monied subjects, contemporary thriller

(Tonal range: from chamber-prestige Downton through ironic-Altman, repression-Ivory, thriller-violent Parasite, to satirical-grotesque Saltburn / Menu. Same membrane, opposite registers.)

## Emotional contract
**Shame + transgression + consequence.** The audience watches the line between served and serving be crossed — by sex, knowledge, ambition, mistake, or violence — and waits for the cost. The pleasure is voyeuristic; the ache is moral. The genre is honest about the violence of class without pretending the audience is above it.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Bell** — Servants summoned, masters' demand, instant obedience; the genre's basic transaction. The bell rings and the lower class moves; the timing IS the membrane.
- **The Servants' Hall / Kitchen** — The lower's interior space, paralleled with the upper's drawing room. The genre lives in cutting between the two.
- **The Trespass** — A specific moment when the Crosser enters a forbidden space — the master bedroom, the library, the family lunch, the front door. Visual + spatial transgression.
- **The Affair Across the Line** — Sex between the classes; the membrane's most charged crossing; consequence is structural, not romantic.
- **The Damning Letter / Note / Photograph** — Physical evidence of the transgression that the Watcher controls. Burnable. Leverageable. The plot's hinge.

**Supporting tropes (stack 3-6 per work):**
- **The Borrowed Dress / Suit** — Crosser puts on the upper's clothes; transformation scene, almost-passing, snap back to position when caught.
- **The Dinner Party** — Set-piece where the membrane is most visible (served and serving in the same room); language coded, eyes glanced.
- **The Christmas / Holiday Episode** — Forced proximity, masters playing at egalitarianism, the membrane snapping back at New Year.
- **The Promotion / Dismissal** — Movement up or down within the lower class; mirrors the upper's hierarchy.
- **The Pregnancy** — Usually the female lower's; consequence enforced by Gatekeeper; sometimes the upper's responsibility, more often denied.
- **The Loyal Lie** — A servant covers for a master's transgression — drinking, affair, theft, fight; the genre's most reliable bond, also the most morally compromised.
- **The Theft** — Object goes missing; suspicion settles by class; the Watcher may know more than they say.
- **The Below-Stairs Romance** — Lower-class courtship paralleled to the dangerous cross-class one; safe variant.
- **The War / Strike / Election** — External world intrudes; the membrane is tested by history; some servants leave, some masters are demobbed.
- **The Speech of Place** — A character (often the Gatekeeper, sometimes the Master) lays out the social order explicitly to the Crosser; warning or invitation.
- **The Long Walk Up the Stairs** — Crosser ascends physically; camera tracks; meets a master at the top.
- **The Sound from the Other Room** — Laughter, music, weeping heard through walls; the membrane is also acoustic.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't reform the class structure. Reform is not the recipe's payoff; consequence is. The genre is honest about social rigidity even when individual characters move.
- Don't make the Crosser purely a victim. They want something; their want is part of what's transgressive. (Saltburn, Parasite, Lady Macbeth lean hard on this.)
- Don't make the Master purely a villain. The charm is real; the harm comes through the same charm. Reduce them to moustache and the genre flattens.
- Don't show the membrane as easy to cross. The genre fails if mobility is frictionless; every crossing must be visibly costly to someone.
- Don't end with the Crosser comfortably integrated into the upper class. Either they are expelled, ruined, transformed-but-marked, or have made the transgression at the cost of a peer.
- Don't moralize from outside. The judgement is dramatized through the Watcher and the Innocent's body, not narrated by the show.
- Don't ignore service work as work. The genre demands competent depiction of what the lower do — the food, the laundry, the polishing, the timetables. Aestheticizing service into decor breaks the recipe.

## Conformity rules
- The household / institution must contain BOTH classes physically — same building, same compound, same restaurant, same yacht. Cross-cuts between separate locations break the form. The membrane must be spatial.
- The Gatekeeper role MUST exist and enforce the order from within the lower class. Without this, the genre collapses to simple upstairs-villainy.
- The transgression must have STRUCTURAL consequence — not just personal heartbreak. Someone loses position, money, reputation, life. The membrane snaps back visibly.
- The Master / Mistress must be CHARMING. The genre relies on the audience understanding the seduction. Cardboard villains break the recipe.
- Service work must be shown competently. Cooking, cleaning, dressing, serving, driving — labor on screen.
- The Innocent's harm must register morally — the show cannot use a child's suffering for plot color without dramatic weight.
- Resolution must NOT integrate the Crosser comfortably into the upper class. The genre's honesty about class requires that the membrane outlasts most attempts to cross it.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Era — Edwardian (Downton, Remains, Gosford), Victorian (Belgravia, Lady Macbeth), interwar (Atonement), 60s (The Servant), contemporary (Parasite, Saltburn, The Menu, You).
- Institution — country house (Downton, Gosford), city household (The Servant, Mrs Wilson), restaurant (The Menu), royal household (The Crown), bookstore/penthouse (You), suburban home (Parasite), university+estate (Saltburn).
- Tonal register — chamber-prestige (Downton, Belgravia), ironic-Altman, repression-Ivory, satirical-grotesque (Saltburn, Menu), thriller-violent (Parasite, Lady Macbeth), psychological-erotic (Servant).
- Direction of the crossing — bottom-up (Crosser is the servant ambitious for the upper — Parasite, Saltburn), top-down (Master breaks the membrane to descend — Lady Chatterley register), mutual (Atonement, Downton).
- National / cultural class structure — British aristocracy, Korean hagwon-bourgeois, US civil-rights-era racial-class hybrid, French ancien régime, etc.
- Whether the show invites identification with the lower (Downton, The Help, Parasite first half) or with the upper (Crown, Gosford), or refuses both (Saltburn, Menu).
- The wiring's edges are fixed (service access enables the trespass; generosity creates evidence; displaced labor harms the Innocent; closing ranks costs the Crosser their position) — every key, note, room, duty, and consequence varies freely.

## Caveats
- The genre's danger is nostalgia. Downton's warmth tilts toward sentiment about service; resisting that requires the Innocent's harm and the Master's closing-of-ranks to land hard. Without them, the recipe becomes lifestyle pornography in period costume.
- The satirical-grotesque end (Saltburn, Menu, Parasite) inverts but does not break the recipe — the membrane is the same; the camera takes the lower's side openly. These are not a different genre; they are the recipe with the polemic foregrounded.
- The Help and similar US racial-class works require care: race is not class, and the recipe's class membrane can mask racial structure if the writer is not deliberate. Apply the recipe AND name the additional axis explicitly.
- Common failure mode: the Master / Mistress becomes a cardboard villain — sneering, mustache-twirling — which lets the audience off the hook. The charm must work. The audience must want to be near them. The horror is that wanting.
- Common failure mode: the membrane is asserted in dialogue but not shown spatially. The genre needs corridors, back stairs, kitchens, servants' halls, separate entrances. Architecture is half the recipe. (Now encoded in the wiring: service access, the back stair, and the household's altered roster.)
- The Watcher is often underwritten — they become a plot device. Strong Watchers (Mrs Hughes, the housekeeper in Gosford, the inspector in The Menu) carry the show's conscience.
