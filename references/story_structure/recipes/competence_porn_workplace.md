# Competence Porn (Workplace)

## Slot
Workplace drama where a TEAM OF SPECIALISTS at a HIGH-STAKES INSTITUTION face PROBLEMS THAT ARRIVE FASTER THAN THEY CAN BE SOLVED, and the pleasure of the show is watching ADULTS WHO KNOW WHAT THEY ARE DOING apply CRAFT under PRESSURE. The institution is the protagonist; the cases are the metronome; the team is the family. The audience leaves wanting to be better at their job.

## Corpus
- *The West Wing* (Sorkin) — White House, walk-and-talk-prestige
- *ER* (Crichton) — county hospital, kinetic-ensemble
- *House M.D.* (Shore) — diagnostic unit, procedural-acid
- *The Newsroom* (Sorkin) — cable news, prestige-polemical
- *Slow Horses* (Apple) — disgraced spies, dry-British-bitter
- *Halt and Catch Fire* (AMC) — tech startup, prestige-historical
- *Sports Night* (Sorkin) — cable sports, foundational-Sorkin
- *Mad Men* (lean) — ad agency, prestige-craft showcased
- *Lessons in Chemistry* (Apple) — 50s chemistry lab + TV studio, period-warm
- *The Pitt* (Max) — ER, real-time-procedural
- *The Diplomat* (Netflix) — ambassadorship, sharp-character
- *Andor* (lean) — rebel cell, prestige-genre
- *Severance* (lean) — corporate, sci-fi-coded but workplace-shaped
- *For All Mankind* (Apple) — alt-history NASA, prestige-genre
- *The Bear* (FX) — restaurant kitchen, chaos-prestige

(Tonal range: from sleek-prestige West Wing through procedural-ER, dry-Slow Horses, kinetic-Bear, period-Lessons. Same recipe — adults applying craft to live problems — different registers of how the institution wears them.)

## Emotional contract
**Mastery + pressure + belonging.** The audience watches people who are GOOD at their work do that work under stakes that matter, in a place that holds them when they fail. The catharsis is competence; the warmth is the team; the ache is what the job costs.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Cold Open Crisis** — Episode opens with the problem already in motion: ambulance bay, breaking news, intelligence flash, kitchen lunch rush, mission briefing. The audience drops into competence in progress. No exposition setup; the team is already moving.
- **The Walk-and-Talk** — Dialogue delivered while moving through institutional corridors; demonstrates expertise in motion, geography of the workplace, density of operations. The genre's signature shot.
- **The Case of the Week** — Episodic problem layered over season arc. The team solves (or fails to solve) ONE specific instance of their craft per episode. Resolution provides the dopamine.
- **The Jargon Drop** — Specialist vocabulary used at speed and not over-explained. The audience does not understand every term and likes it that way — feels like overhearing pros.
- **The Crisis Pull-Together** — A scene where the team converges around one problem — over the table, around a whiteboard, in the operating bay, at the bench. Spatial concentration as emotional payoff.

**Supporting tropes (stack 3-6 per work):**
- **The Late Night Office** — Lights low, half the team gone home, two characters left finishing the work; intimacy without romance (usually); the show's confession booth.
- **The Mentor Lecture** — The Lead or Boss teaches a younger team member; ostensibly about craft, actually about life. Rare, valuable, must not turn into a TED talk.
- **The Bottle Episode** — Whole episode confined to the workplace; pressure cooker; bottle-of-truth feel. Genre's stress test.
- **The Outside Crisis Lands** — A specific case lands that intersects with a character's personal life — patient is their ex's mother, source is the Lead's old friend, political problem touches their family. The case becomes personal.
- **The Bureaucratic Wall** — Admin, board, oversight, parent agency, regulator, health department obstructs the team's work. The Boss runs interference.
- **The After-Shift Drink** — Bar, late-night diner, breakroom, the kitchen station after close; debrief and bond. Where the team becomes a team.
- **The Big Speech** — A character makes the case for the institution's purpose — the West Wing's defining gesture; the Newsroom's; The Bear's father-speech equivalent. Use sparingly; the genre needs them but cannot survive on them.
- **The Quiet Save** — Someone notices something small that turns the case — a chart anomaly, a mistranslation, a missing ingredient, an unstated motive. The genre's emotional currency.
- **The Failure** — A case that does not solve; the team grieves; the institution rolls on. At least one per season is required.
- **The Outside Crisis Lands** — A real-world historical event (or its fictional equivalent) intrudes; the team has to do their job inside a bigger story.
- **The Personal Subplot** — A character's marriage, illness, child, or addiction tracks across the season alongside the cases. The job's cost made visible.
- **The Specialist Cameo** — A guest expert from another department / institution arrives, demonstrates a different facet of the craft, leaves. Pace variation.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't make incompetence the source of comedy from a regular cast member. Comic incompetence is for guest characters (the inept lawyer, the bad intern). Regulars must be GOOD at their jobs even when they fail.
- Don't have the team win every case. Failure must land at least once a season; the genre's honesty about institutional limits is the floor.
- Don't have the Lead Specialist solve every case themselves. The team must matter; collaboration is the recipe. House MD bends this; Slow Horses corrects it.
- Don't moralize the institution as pure good. The Bureaucratic Wall, the Adversarial Counterpart, and the institutional cost are part of the recipe; sanitizing the workplace breaks it.
- Don't over-explain the jargon. The audience earns belonging by puzzling some of it out. Glossary scenes belong in pilots only, and even then sparingly.
- Don't resolve the personal subplot in one episode. Marriage trouble, addiction, grief move across the season at the pace of life.
- Don't turn the Outsider Hire into the protagonist by mid-season unless the show is deliberately doing the handoff. The audience came for the Lead's craft.

## Conformity rules
- The team must be COMPETENT. Regulars must do their job well most of the time. Failure is dramatic event; incompetence is genre-breaking.
- The institution must be specific — a named hospital, agency, restaurant, network, White House office, lab. Not "a hospital" — Cook County General. Specificity is craft.
- At least one case must fail or partially fail per season. The genre's honesty floor.
- The personal subplots must run alongside the cases; cases without personal cost are procedural, not workplace drama. Workplace drama requires that the job moves the people too.
- The Late Night Office or its equivalent (the empty kitchen at 1am, the after-shift bar) must appear regularly. The intimate institutional space is the genre's emotional engine.
- The jargon must be present, fast, and not over-explained. The audience's pleasure includes feeling slightly behind.
- The Boss must serve as the membrane between team and outside world; the team's exposure to bureaucratic pressure must be dramatized, not skipped.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Institution — White House (West Wing), hospital (ER, House, The Pitt), newsroom (Newsroom, Sports Night), spy agency (Slow Horses, Diplomat), restaurant (The Bear), startup (Halt and Catch Fire), ad agency (Mad Men), space program (For All Mankind), lab (Lessons in Chemistry), rebel cell (Andor), severed-floor corporate (Severance).
- Tonal register — sleek-prestige (West Wing), kinetic (ER, The Bear, The Pitt), dry-bitter (Slow Horses), period-warm (Lessons), acid-procedural (House), polemical (Newsroom).
- Case cadence — pure case-of-the-week (early ER, House), heavy-serial-with-cases (Halt, Slow Horses, West Wing), almost-pure serial with institutional shape (Andor, Severance).
- The Lead Specialist's domain — clinical, political, journalistic, intelligence, culinary, scientific, creative, technical, military. The craft is what changes.
- Whether the institution is morally clean (West Wing-ish), morally compromised (Slow Horses, Halt), or actively corrupt (Severance, Andor's empire). The team's relationship to the institution is a register dial.
- Speed of the room — Sorkin-fast vs Bear-frantic vs Slow Horses-slouched. The craft can be performed at any tempo.

## Variation rules (additional — pace and tempo)
- Real-time (The Pitt) versus episodic-week (ER) versus year-spanning (Mad Men, Halt). Time scale is a free dial as long as the case cadence is preserved within whatever unit.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the failed case creates the gap, the bottle burns procedure, the outside crisis brings scrutiny, the final case consumes the missing method and the confession) — the content of every edge varies with the institution.

## Caveats
- The genre's primary failure mode is preachiness. The Big Speech is a tool, not a structure. Sorkin shows mastered the speech in 30 seconds and the show in 60 minutes; imitators flip the ratio.
- "Quirky workplace" comedy (Brooklyn Nine-Nine, Parks and Rec) is a sibling genre but NOT this slot — those are sitcoms in workplaces. Competence porn requires that the work itself be the spectacle and the stakes be real. Lives, freedom, dignity, business survival, broadcast credibility.
- The Brilliant Difficult One is the role most often over-indexed — the show becomes The House Show, The Saga of the Asshole Genius. Slow Horses corrects this by spreading the difficulty across the team. The Lead Specialist should not always be the difficult one.
- The genre is institution-loving by default. Hard cynicism about the institution (Slow Horses, Severance, Andor) bends the form productively; full cynicism breaks it — the team must believe in the work even when the institution does not.
- Common failure mode: the institution becomes a backdrop and the cases stop having craft texture. The genre demands that the work be specific — the labwork, the diagnostic sequence, the news broadcast cadence, the surgical instruments. Vague competence is no competence.
- Common failure mode: personal subplots overwhelm the cases. The show drifts to relationship drama with a workplace set. Symptom: episode passes without the team solving anything. Correction: case discipline; restore the Case of the Week.
