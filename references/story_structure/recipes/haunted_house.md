# Haunted House (Grief + Dread + Release)

## Slot
Serialized horror where a HOUSE holds a wound — a death, a buried crime, a stolen child, an unfinished mourning — and a PROTAGONIST carrying their own unresolved grief moves in (or returns). The house responds to the wound it recognizes. The season is the slow conversation between two griefs: the house's and the protagonist's. Catharsis arrives only when the protagonist names what they have been refusing to feel; the haunting either releases them, claims them, or is claimed by them.

Same recipe — the house holds a wound; the new arrival's grief is the key.)

## Emotional contract
**Grief + dread + release.** The audience watches a wounded protagonist enter a house that knows their wound, then suffer alongside them as the house externalizes the buried thing, then exhale (or grieve harder) when the secret is named. Catharsis is not safety — it is recognition. The recipe runs across registers because the engine is psychological: every register stages "the house knows what you have not said yet."

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Fresh Start** — The Protagonist enters the House for stated reasons that are not grief: new job, new marriage, cheap rent, inherited property, retreat. The grief is the unstated reason. The audience reads both.
- **The First Wrong Thing** — Within the first episode, one small wrongness lands — a sound, a door, a temperature, a flicker, a moved object, a child's drawing of someone who isn't there. The recipe's covenant signal.
- **The Mirror Wound** — The house's wound and the Protagonist's wound resonate. Dead child in the family / dead child in the house. Drowned wife / unprocessed marriage. Buried fire / buried rage. Specificity matters; the mirror must be exact.
- **The Naming Scene** — Late-season scene in which the Protagonist says aloud the thing they have been refusing to feel. Often direct address — to the ghost, to the child, to the dark. The recipe's spine moment.
- **The Final Negotiation** — Climactic encounter with the haunting — exorcism, mediumship, fire, escape, fight, surrender, ritual. The Protagonist either releases the ghost, is taken by it, or assumes its role.

**Supporting tropes:**
- **The Real-Estate Bargain** — House is suspiciously affordable / available / inherited. The price is signposted; the Skeptic dismisses it.
- **The Family Photograph (Old)** — Photo or portrait of the prior household discovered in the attic, basement, or estate file. Frames the prior wound.
- **The Child's Imaginary Friend** — The vulnerable member talks to someone the adults cannot see. Drawings, conversations, gifts. Recipe's earliest reliable signal.
- **The Cold Spot / The Smell** — Sensory wrongness recurring in one specific room — temperature drop, perfume, rot, woodsmoke. The audience learns the room's grammar.
- **The Attic / The Basement / The Locked Room** — Architectural specificity — one part of the House is more itself than the rest. By midseason, the Protagonist has been there. By finale, they will be there at full intensity.
- **The Cassette / The Tape / The Found Footage** — Recorded medium captures what was missed live — voice, figure, knock. Validates the Protagonist to the Skeptic.
- **The Investigator's Briefing** — Scene in which the medium / priest / historian lays out the framework — the house's history, the rules, the rite. Permits the audience to track stakes.
- **The Ritual Object** — Crucifix, sage, salt circle, ouija board, talisman, family heirloom. The intervention's material vocabulary.
- **The Wrong Sound from the Other Room** — Domestic sound coming from where no one is — laughter, footsteps, piano, music box, water running. The house's signature.
- **The Possession Lapse** — A character (often the Skeptic, sometimes the Protagonist) acts against their nature briefly — voice change, time lost, violent impulse. The house tested them.
- **The Animal Sees It** — Dog, cat, horse reacts to invisible presences. Cheap, reliable, audiences love it.
- **The Boundary Breach** — Late escalation — the haunting follows them out of the house, into the car, into another room, into the body. The contract widens.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't introduce the ghost without a wound. A haunting without grief is just a monster in a house. The mirror is the recipe.
- Don't let the Protagonist enter the House with no internal weight. If they are not carrying something the House recognizes, the season has no heat.
- Don't explain the haunting through one long monologue from the Historian. The history must be assembled across episodes — photo, tape, drawing, briefing, witness, attic visit.
- Don't make the Skeptic a fool. They must be sympathetic enough that their disbelief is understandable. Otherwise the gaslight cost is unearned.
- Don't kill the vulnerable member as a cheap stake-raiser. If they die, the death must rearrange the Protagonist — not just goose the tension.
- Don't end with safe domestic restoration if grief was the engine. The Conjuring's "house cleared, family hugs" ending only works because Lorraine carries the wound forward. Pure restoration without cost flattens the recipe.
- Don't make the House merely evil. The House is in pain. Even Hill House — the apex example — is a wounded creature, not a malevolent intelligence.

## Conformity rules
- The House must be specific, named, mappable, and architecturally consistent. The audience must learn the floorplan; the camera must return.
- The Protagonist must carry a specific grief. Vague unease is the wrong genre. Name the wound to the audience by Episode 2 at the latest.
- The Mirror Wound must be exact. House's child / Protagonist's child. House's marriage / Protagonist's marriage. House's fire / Protagonist's rage. Specificity is the recipe.
- The Vulnerable Member must see it first. The hierarchy of belief (child believes, adult denies, expert confirms) is the genre's pulse.
- The history must be ASSEMBLED — photo + neighbor + tape + briefing + attic. Do not monologue the worldbuilding.
- The Naming Scene must occur. If the Protagonist never says the buried thing aloud, the recipe has not run.
- The ending must have weight. Release / claim / replace — pick one; commit. No clean drives-away-untouched endings. The House marks whoever leaves.

- Failure mode: the haunting without the grief. If the Protagonist is unwounded, the House has no leverage and the season becomes monster-of-the-week.
- Failure mode: the Investigator over-explains. The framework should arrive late and partial. If Episode 3 is a lecture, the assembly mechanic dies.
- Failure mode: the Vulnerable Member as plot device. Their interiority matters — drawings, fears, questions. If they exist only to scream, the recipe leaks.
- Failure mode: the ending as safety. Conjuring-style "house cleared" only works if Lorraine carries the wound forward (and the franchise does). Self-contained stories need release that marks, not erases.
- Failure mode: the Skeptic as villain. The Skeptic is the audience's denial made visible. They must be loved (or at least understood) for the conversion to land.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- The wound type — dead child (most common; powerful), buried spouse, prior crime, displaced inhabitants, stolen child, suicide, slow institutional violence (Bly, Hill House). Pick the wound the Protagonist's specific grief mirrors.
- Register — pulp-jump-scare (Conjuring, Insidious), prestige-Flanagan, art-meditative (Lowery, Aster lean), comic (Beetlejuice), period (Crimson Peak, Others, Innocents). Recipe runs across all.
- Whose POV — woman alone (Others, Babadook), nuclear family (Conjuring, Poltergeist, Amityville), siblings returning (Hill House), couple (Crimson Peak, Insidious), one Protagonist + dog (A Ghost Story inverse). Household composition is dial.
- Ending — released, claimed, replaced. All three valid; the choice declares the season's argument about grief.
- Whether the haunting is real or psychological. Babadook leans psychological; Hereditary lands supernatural; Others inverts; Shining holds both. Pick the register's clarity deliberately.
- Era — historical (Crimson Peak, Others, Innocents), period (Hill House's flashbacks, Conjuring's '70s), contemporary (Insidious, Paranormal Activity, A Ghost Story's contemporary opening). Era is dial.
- House type — Victorian estate, suburban tract, apartment (Sinister leans toward "house"), motel, mansion, farm. The architecture inflects the wound's flavor.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (denial seals the room; withheld history breaks the ritual; mapped architecture and named grief enable the negotiation) — every wound, route, artifact, and ending varies freely.

## Caveats
- Register dial: pulp-jump-scare runs at five wrong-things per episode; prestige-Flanagan runs at one wrong-thing per episode with extended grief scenes; art-meditative runs at half a wrong-thing per episode with the camera as ghost. All three honor the recipe.
- Genre proximity: gothic romance pulls toward a person inside the house; haunted house pulls toward the house itself. If the desire object is a living person, drift to gothic romance. If the desire object is the dead, you are home.
- Genre proximity: domestic horror puts the abuser INSIDE the household and uses gaslight without supernatural agency. Haunted house externalizes the wound into architecture. The line is whether the antagonist sleeps in the bed beside you (domestic) or is in the walls (haunted).
