# Cursed Gunslinger Western (anime-horror variant)

## Slot
Genre-fused serialized story (animation native; live-action with anime sensibility also works) where a cursed or supernaturally-marked gunslinger walks a frontier of undead, demons, or cosmic horror, working toward a final reckoning that the curse demands. Western iconography (dust, frontier, six-shooters, lone-rider entry) layered onto anime kinetic-emotional register and horror's body-cost. Project-specific recipe — Hellmouth Cowboy / gunslinger projects live here. For the plain non-supernatural Western, see `western.md`.

- *Trigun* (Nightow / Madhouse)
- *Cowboy Bebop* (Watanabe / Sunrise)
- *Vampire Hunter D: Bloodlust* (Kawajiri)
- *Hellsing Ultimate* (Madhouse)
- *Bone Tomahawk* (Zahler)

- *El Topo* (Jodorowsky)
- *Dead Man* (Jarmusch)
- *From Dusk Till Dawn* (Rodriguez / Tarantino)
- *Mandy* (Cosmatos)
- *The Northman* (Eggers)
- *Spaghetti Western adjacents* (Leone — for the iconography baseline)
- *Berserk* (Miura — for the cursed-protagonist quest engine)
- *Castlevania* (Netflix)

(Tonal range: Trigun's comic-tragic through Bebop's elegiac-cool through Bone Tomahawk's body-horror brutal to Mandy's psychedelic-vengeful. Same engine, different dials.)

## Emotional contract
**Vengeance + dread + reckoning.** The viewer rides with a protagonist who is already half-dead, watches the body count and the personal cost mount, and pays at the destination — not with a happy ending but with a reckoning that the curse demanded. The catharsis is in earned violence and the elegiac stillness that follows.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (the engine):**
- **The Curse Object** — A physical thing the Gunslinger carries that anchors the curse: a brand, a gun, a coin, a relic, a hair. Visible. Touched in moments of doubt.
- **The Slow Walk** — At least once per episode (often the opener), the Gunslinger walks into a place quietly, alone, while everyone stops and looks. The walk IS the threat.
- **The First Bullet** — The protagonist's skill is established within the first 5 minutes of the pilot — a SINGLE shot that does an impossible thing. Sets the body-count baseline.
- **The Dust-Caked Town** — Each episode lands in a small-town setting (saloon, dirt street, train station, frontier outpost) — the unit of episodic structure.
- **The Showdown at High Noon** — At least one episode ends in a direct confrontation under open sky. The genre's signature image.
- **The Hellmouth Whisper** — Periodic flashes (visions, sounds, broken radio, ghost-speech) reminding the audience of the destination.
- **The Body Count Mounts** — Each episode adds to a running tally — bodies, towns, allies lost. The audience tracks it.
- **The Companion's Last Words** — When the Companion dies (or nearly does), they get a single line — usually a question the Gunslinger can't answer.
- **The Curse Mechanic Manifests** — Once per episode, the curse does something specific (the gun fires by itself, the body heals impossibly, the Gunslinger's reflection moves wrong). Visible reminder.

**Supporting tropes (stack 3-5 per season):**
- **The Bounty** — A poster appears in a town with the Gunslinger's face on it; he goes about his business; the bounty hunters die.
- **The Drink in Silence** — Shared drink between Gunslinger and a former enemy, hostage, or witness. No dialogue, or minimal dialogue. The pause between violences.
- **The Hellmouth Mark** — The Gunslinger's body shows the curse — a scar, a missing piece, an eye, a tattoo. Visible to other cursed beings.
- **The Old Photograph** — A keepsake of the Wronged Innocent appears at moments of doubt. The Gunslinger pulls it out, looks, puts it away.
- **The Sermon** — A priest or charlatan delivers a sermon that the Gunslinger sits through; the sermon IS the episode's thematic argument.
- **The Cursed Town** — A whole town is corrupted (vampires masquerading as residents, a demon's chorus, the dead refusing to stay dead). The Gunslinger arrives, the town is cleansed, the cost is the town itself.
- **The Mirror Episode** — One mid-season episode where the Gunslinger meets a version of himself (a younger man following the same path, a ghost of the man he was). The episode pivots the season.
- **The Train / The Coach** — Transit between towns. Often where the Companion appears.
- **The Antagonist's Speech** — At the threshold of the Hellmouth, the Antagonist (or its avatar) gets a monologue that almost convinces the Gunslinger to turn back. He doesn't.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't make the Gunslinger a noble hero. He's already damned. He's not redeeming the West; he's walking through it.
- Don't redeem the Gunslinger through love. The Companion is not a love interest to be married. If romance happens, it ends in loss or denial.
- Don't have the Antagonist surrender or repent. The Hellmouth doesn't apologize.
- Don't end with a wedding, a town saved, a peaceful retirement. Endings are reckonings, not restorations.
- Don't make the curse explainable through psychology. It's metaphysical, not metaphorical-only.
- Don't have the Gunslinger talk too much. Silence is the genre's default register. Dialogue is currency; spend it.

## Conformity rules
- **Western iconography is non-negotiable.** Dust, frontier landscape, six-shooters or their equivalent, lone-rider entry into towns. If the surface is space-western or post-apocalyptic, the iconography still translates.
- **The protagonist is CURSED, not chosen.** The curse is the engine; the protagonist did not volunteer for this.
- **The horror element must be MATERIAL on screen.** Undead, demons, cosmic horror, ghost-flesh — not psychological-only. The genre's pillars are kinetic + body-cost.
- **The Hellmouth must be established by Ep 5.** The destination's identity is the season's spine; without it the show wanders.
- **The Body Count Mounts.** Each episode adds to a tally the audience tracks. Hidden body count violates the audience's contract.
- **Silence is the default register.** Dialogue is rationed; the genre's currency is what's NOT said.
- **The ending is a RECKONING, not a restoration.** No town is "saved." No marriage. No peaceful retirement. The reckoning is the catharsis.
- **The Companion's last words are answerable only by action.** The Gunslinger doesn't reply to grief with speech.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- **Specific horror flavor** — zombie outbreak (Trigun-adjacent), vampire (Vampire Hunter D), cosmic (Mandy), demonic (Berserk, Castlevania), ghost (Dead Man).
- **Curse mechanic** — gun-bound (the gun is the curse), blood-bound (a wound that won't heal), ancestor-bound (a family debt), oath-bound (a vow that animates the body).
- **Companion type** — child (Lone Wolf and Cub adjacent), priestess (Vampire Hunter D), former-enemy (Hellsing adjacent), hostage-turned-ally.
- **Tonal register** — comic-tragic (Trigun), elegiac (Bebop, Dead Man), brutal (Bone Tomahawk, Mandy), psychedelic (El Topo, Mandy).
- **Setting layer** — historical-frontier American West; post-apocalyptic-frontier; space-frontier; Edo-period (jidaigeki cross); steampunk.
- **The Hellmouth's nature** — geographic (a place to ride to), temporal (a deadline), bodily (the curse is the Hellmouth — the Gunslinger IS the destination), institutional (a corrupt city / church / corporation).
- **Whether the curse breaks** — sometimes yes (at terrible cost), sometimes no (the genre tolerates either).
- **The wiring's edges are fixed** (the road emits a wound, a promise, a map, and a toll that the threshold and reckoning must consume) — the CONTENT of every edge varies freely: whose dead, what the Hellmouth wants, how the body pays, whether the last ride is release or sentence.

## Caveats
- **The genre tolerates wide register variation.** Trigun-comic and Bone Tomahawk-brutal hit the same beats. Register is the dial above the recipe.
- **Anime + Western fusion specifically expects** the genre's kinetic register (deliberate framing, charged silences, eye-and-face emphasis) to apply to Western iconography. Plain Western iconography in standard live-action register is a different slot.
- **The Hellmouth design** is the season's marketing identity. It must be a specific, namable, evocative endpoint — not a vague "evil rises."
- **Common failure mode:** making the Gunslinger too redemptive. He cannot become a noble hero — he can become a person who PAID the cost, which is different.
- **Common failure mode:** explaining the curse psychologically. The metaphysical layer must persist; the curse is real, not a coping mechanism for trauma.
- **Common failure mode:** the body count being hidden / off-screen / morally clean. The genre requires visible cost.
- Project-specific note: gunslinger_v12 brief lists "a cursed gunslinger in a world of undead, who uses violence to silence his literal and figurative demons while seeking the Hellmouth to end it all" — this maps cleanly to the recipe. The pipeline's outputs should hit these beats.
