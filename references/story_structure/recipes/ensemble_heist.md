# Ensemble Heist

## Slot
A CREW with complementary specialties is assembled by a planner to TAKE a valuable target (vault, painting, score, identity, secret) under time and risk pressure. The plan goes wrong; the crew improvises; the REVERSAL reveals that the plan we watched was a layer of a deeper plan. The pleasure is competence, camaraderie, and being fooled in a way that flatters the audience.

- *Money Heist / La Casa de Papel* (Netflix / Antena 3)
- *Lupin* (Netflix)
- *Leverage* (TNT)

## Emotional contract
**Cleverness + camaraderie + reversal.** The audience watches a crew of specialists be brilliant together, gets the warmth of a chosen family executing under pressure, and is rewarded for trusting the show with a final reveal that recasts what they thought they saw.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The Assembly Sequence** — Crew is recruited one by one; each Specialist gets a signature introduction showing their competence in their domain. The audience meets the team through what they can do.
- **The Plan-as-Shown** — The crew's plan is dramatized as a montage or briefing: precise steps, contingencies, timing. The audience trusts they're seeing the whole thing.
- **The Complication** — Mid-runtime, the plan breaks. A guard moves unexpectedly, a code rotates, a Specialist's flaw triggers, the Mark shifts schedule, the Authority closes in. The crew improvises.
- **The Reversal** — Final-act reveal that the plan we watched was a layer of a deeper plan the Planner kept from the crew (and from the audience). The Complication was either expected or arranged.

**Supporting tropes (stack 3-6 per work):**
- **The Recruit Refuses, Then Joins** — One Specialist initially declines the job (retired, debt-free, in a relationship). They are turned by either a personal angle or by the score being too good. Their reluctance becomes their reliability.
- **The Banter Beat** — At least one scene per act where the crew jokes over coffee / drinks / in transit. The camaraderie that makes the audience love them happens here, not in the heist itself.
- **The Specialist's Tool** — Each Specialist has signature equipment — a specific drill, a brand of headset, a deck of cards, a particular car. The tools become the show's iconography.
- **The Casing Sequence** — The crew studies the target through surveillance, blueprints, social engineering. Often the show's exposition of the location's geography (which will pay off in the Complication and the Reversal).
- **The Disguise Sequence** — A Specialist (often the Face) impersonates an authority — security guard, executive, technician, cleric — to enter the target. Costume change as competence demonstration.
- **The Almost-Caught** — A scene where the Authority or the Mark nearly closes in mid-job. Pulse beat; the crew improvises out.
- **The Crew-Member-in-Crisis** — One Specialist is tested by their named flaw mid-runtime — the addict near a stash, the gambler near a table, the lover near the Mark's daughter. They wobble or fail; the crew compensates.
- **The Insider Tilts** — The Inside Man / Woman is leveraged, threatened, or has a crisis of conscience; their cooperation becomes uncertain. The crew has to either re-flip them or work around them.
- **The Fake Mark** — A character introduced as a fellow conspirator or rival turns out to be either an undercover authority or another player running their own version of the same heist.
- **The Split** — Late in the runtime, the crew separates physically. Crosscutting between locations builds the climax tempo.
- **The Score Reveal** — The audience learns what the target actually was. Sometimes it's not what we were told (Inside Man); sometimes it's the smaller piece of a larger play (Ocean's 12; Money Heist).
- **The Walk-Away Shot** — Final image — the crew leaves the city, splits the take, sits in a beach bar, drives away. Glamour or weariness; either fits.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't kill the Specialists. The crew should survive. One death is allowable in darker variants (Rififi); more than one tips the genre toward crime drama.
- Don't make the Mark sympathetic enough that the crew is wrong to steal from them. The genre is class-aligned with the crew. Marks are arrogant, complicit, or actively villainous.
- Don't reveal the Reversal too early. The audience must believe the Plan-as-Shown was the whole plan. A premature reveal kills the third-act pleasure.
- Don't end with the crew in jail unless the show is doing dark-Bandits / Rififi explicitly. The default ending is escape with the score.
- Don't make the Specialists fungible. Each must own a specialty no one else can do. If a Specialist's job could be done by another member, the role isn't differentiated enough.
- Don't end with the Planner alone. The camaraderie is the contract; the closing image is the crew (or at least a pair) together.
- Don't introduce a romance that doesn't serve the heist. Romantic subplots either drive a Specialist's flaw, anchor the New Recruit, or pay off in the Reversal — never decoration.
- Don't have the heist succeed without the Complication. The Plan-as-Shown executing cleanly with no improvisation is dramatically inert.

The small job surfaces the key to a much bigger target, named by episode's end. The signature is not vanity, it is bait: it starts the Authority building a profile. **Needs:** —. **Sets up:** E1-calling-card (→E5, E10), E1-target-key (→E2, E4). | Charm. | 2 | **The Assembly Begins.** Because the target surfaced in E1 demands skills no one person has, the Planner recruits the Second and the first Specialists — and every recruitment BUYS a liability: the gambler comes with the debt, the refuser is turned by a personal angle that is now owed. The Recruit Refuses, Then Joins lands at least once. The crew that exists by episode's end still has one hole no one in it can fill. **Needs:** E1-target-key. **Sets up:** E2-flaw-liability (→E7), E2-personal-angle (→E10), E2-missing-specialty (→E3). | Pleasure of meeting smart people. |

First crew dinner; the banter establishes what the crew now stands to lose in each other. Tools are seen. **Needs:** E2-missing-specialty. **Sets up:** E3-recruit-access (→E4), E3-crew-bond (→E8). | Camaraderie's first warmth. |

The Planner briefs the Plan-as-Shown and — on screen, as a choice — omits the deepest layer. The Second notices the briefing doesn't add up and chooses to cover it. ## Conformity rules
- The crew must be functionally complementary. Every Specialist owns a specialty no other crew member can do.
- The Reversal is non-negotiable. A heist that plays exactly as the Plan-as-Shown is not in this slot.
- The Complication must happen and must be improvised through. No clean executions.
- Camaraderie is the show's heart. The Banter Beat is required.
- The Mark must be the kind of person the audience is happy to see lose. Sympathetic Marks break the contract.
- The crew survives (in default variants). The closing image includes at least two crew members alive and together.
- The Specialist's Tool — each crew member's signature equipment or technique — must be specific and visible.
- The Authority / Cop must be competent enough to be a real threat. A bumbling cop deflates the genre.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- The target — bank, casino, museum, government records vault, painting, election, identity, secret, person.
- Tonal register — slick-glamour (Ocean's), TV-craftsman (Leverage), working-class-grit (Logan Lucky), operatic-political (Money Heist), elegant-classic (Rififi).
- The Reversal's mechanism — the plan was bigger (Ocean's), the score was different (Inside Man), the crew was bigger (Money Heist), the Mark was the target (The Sting), the heist was a cover for revenge (The Limey rare hybrid).
- The Planner's character — debonair (Ocean), tragic-political (Professor / Money Heist), working-class-savvy (Logan), gentleman-thief (Lupin), academic (Now You See Me).
- The crew's class composition — high-glamour international, blue-collar regional, ex-cops-turned-grifters, magicians, criminals-with-cause.
- The period — modern, 60s-70s caper, period-piece classic, near-future tech-heist.
- Single-job vs serialized — film does one job; TV does either case-of-the-week (Leverage, Hustle) or one mega-job stretched across a season (Money Heist).
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the Complication consumes a planted liability; the Reversal consumes the omission, the bait, and the gamble) — the CONTENT of every edge varies freely: which flaw, what bait, whose humiliation, what the trust costs.

## Caveats
- The Reversal is the hardest single element to write. A weak reversal feels like a cheat; a strong one feels inevitable in retrospect. (Its groundwork is now encoded in the wiring: E1's bait, E4's omitted layer, E5's dark channel, E8's gamble.)
- Common failure mode: a crew where two Specialists do similar things. If the safecracker and the hacker can both bypass the vault, one of them is redundant; the recipe demands functional differentiation.
- Common failure mode: the Mark as a cartoon. They should be competent and arrogant; their loss must feel earned, not deflated. Underwriting the Mark cheapens the heist.
- Register dial: how much politics? Money Heist treats the heist as anti-state action; Ocean's treats it as glamour. The recipe holds across both; the volume of the class / political subtext is the variation.
- The "Heist Goes Wrong, Everyone Dies" subvariant (Rififi, Reservoir Dogs) is technically heist but tonally a different recipe — the camaraderie contract is broken. Use that variant deliberately and price the tonal cost.
- Genre-of-one risk: none. One of cinema and TV's most reliable slots; the recipe is durable across decades and languages.
