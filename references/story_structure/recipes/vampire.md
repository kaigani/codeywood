# Vampire (Desire + Seduction + Damnation)

## Slot
Serialized horror-romance where a VAMPIRE — beautiful, old, hungry — enters the orbit of a MORTAL whose blood, mind, or innocence is exactly what the vampire wants. The vampire is desirable; the desire has a cost; the cost is identity, soul, or future. Whether the register is Eggers-horror, Twilight-romance, or WWDITS-comedy, the recipe is one engine: the bite changes who you are, and someone in the story wants to be bitten.

## Corpus
- *Dracula* (Stoker / Coppola / Eggers' *Nosferatu*)
- *Interview with the Vampire* (Rice / Jordan film / AMC series)
- *Twilight* (Meyer / Hardwicke)
- *The Vampire Diaries* (CW)
- *True Blood* (HBO / Harris)
- *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* (vampire-arc episodes — Angel, Spike)
- *Blade* (Norrington)
- *Nosferatu* (Murnau / Eggers)
- *Let the Right One In* (Lindqvist / Alfredson)
- *What We Do in the Shadows* (Waititi / Clement film + FX series)
- *Only Lovers Left Alive* (Jarmusch)
- *A Discovery of Witches* (Harkness / Sky)
- *Carmilla* (Le Fanu)
- *The Lost Boys* (Schumacher)
- *Underworld* (Wiseman)

(Tonal range: Eggers' folk-horror-bestial and Let the Right One In's Nordic-grim through Interview's gothic-decadent and Only Lovers Left Alive's art-melancholy to Twilight's high-school-romance, True Blood's Southern-pulp, and WWDITS's mockumentary-comic. Same recipe — beautiful predator, mortal flame, the bite that costs.)

## Emotional contract
**Desire + seduction + damnation.** The audience falls for the vampire alongside the mortal, then watches the cost arrive — not as a twist but as the slow accumulation of what feeding for centuries does. Catharsis is the mortal choosing turning, refusing turning, or being unable to choose. The recipe runs across registers because the cocktail is genre-stable: the bite is sex, the sire-bond is family, the long life is grief.

## Trope vocabulary

**Core (genre backbone):**
- **The First Look** — The Vampire sees the Mortal in a public space (a club, a class, a market, a road) and reacts visibly to the smell / sight / sound of them. The audience knows immediately. The Mortal does not yet.
- **The Threshold / The Invitation** — Vampires must be invited in (or its inversion — they breach the line). The recipe's signature negotiation of consent. Door, window, room, body — the invitation escalates by stage.
- **The Bite as Sex** — Feeding scenes are filmed and scored as sex scenes — heart rate, breath, the moment of penetration, recovery. The bite is the genre's primary intimate act regardless of register.
- **The Sire-Bond** — The maker-made relationship is permanent, jealous, and structural. New vampires belong to their sire; freedom is negotiated, not given. Family-of-blood that cannot be left.
- **The Cost of Turning** — Becoming a vampire is portrayed as both ecstasy and grief — gaining hunger, eternity, beauty; losing daylight, mortality, family, growth. The conversion scene is a death scene with sequel.

**Supporting tropes:**
- **The Long Memory** — Flashback to the Vampire's mortal life or earlier century. Establishes the loneliness, the original wound, why this Mortal lands differently.
- **The Old House / The Hotel / The Compound** — The Vampire's residence — gothic estate, decaying hotel, modernist penthouse, hidden chamber. Wealth accumulated across centuries; aesthetic shows the recipe's register.
- **The Day Restriction** — Sunlight as kill switch (or burn / weakness / sparkle depending on register). Schedules the romance to night; the daylight scenes belong to the Mortal's other life.
- **The Mirror Test** — Vampire's relationship to mirror / photograph / video — absent reflection or strange reflection. Reveals what the Vampire is to the Mortal for the first time.
- **The Hunger Lapse** — The Vampire loses control once on screen — kills the wrong person, almost feeds on the Mortal, frightens the Anchor. The cost made undeniable.
- **The Coven Introduction** — The Mortal is brought to meet the Vampire's family. Dinner scene that is also a vetting. Power asymmetry visible.
- **The Hunter's Pursuit** — The Knower appears, traces the kills, closes in. Crosses with the Mortal's life. Forces the Mortal to choose a side.
- **The Blood Pact** — Mortal voluntarily bleeds for the Vampire — wrist, neck, vial, ritual. First mutual act; consent recoded as devotion.
- **The Coffin / The Earth** — Vampire's sleeping arrangement — coffin, soil from homeland, blackout room. Vulnerability scene; the Mortal sees the predator asleep.
- **The Anniversary** — Recurring date — the date of the Vampire's turning, the date of a great loss, a centennial. Marks the Vampire's grief calendar; the Mortal eventually shares one.
- **The Slayer's Cross-Pursuit** — A second vampire (older, more violent, less restrained) appears and treats the Mortal as food. Forces the Lead Vampire to defend their claim.
- **The Refusal** — A moment, often late, when the Mortal is offered the turn and declines (or accepts). The decision is the season's verdict.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't make the Vampire purely monstrous. If they are only predator, the seduction has no traction. The desire must be earned by character, not just by costume.
- Don't make the Mortal purely innocent. They are drawn — for transcendence, for grief-escape, for the recognition the Vampire gives them. Hunger meets hunger.
- Don't cure vampirism cleanly. Cures, if they exist, cost — Angel's curse, Bill's ghost, the half-life of Underworld. No clean reset.
- Don't let the Vampire age the Mortal up. The asymmetry of years is the genre's ache — eight centuries vs eighteen years, ten thousand vs thirty. The math is part of the cocktail.
- Don't withhold the bite to protect a PG rating. The bite IS the contract; staging it euphemistically signals the recipe was not run. Even WWDITS films its bites with care.
- Don't make the Hunter wrong without making them human. Their grief should be earned. If they are cartoon, the moral architecture collapses.
- Don't end on permanent destruction without weight. Killing the Vampire forecloses the genre's signature ache. If the season needs death, mourn it.

## Conformity rules
- The Vampire must be desirable. Not necessarily handsome — Eggers' Orlok is hideous and desirable as appetite. The pull is the engine.
- The bite must be staged as intimate, not incidental. If feeding is throwaway, the cocktail leaks. Even in comedy register, the bite carries weight.
- The cost of turning must be shown, not merely stated. Hunger, loneliness, watching mortals age, daylight loss — at least three of these on screen across the season.
- The Hunter / Knower must be morally legible. Their grief, faith, or family loss must justify their pursuit. Cartoon hunters break the symmetry.
- The Sire-bond or Coven must exist. Pure isolated vampire reads as monster horror; the vampire's social system is what marks the recipe.
- The Mortal's choice in the finale must be real and consequential — turning, refusing, dying, or being turned against will. Ambiguous shrug-endings break the contract.
- The Vampire must have a long memory. At least one flashback / disclosed century anchors the asymmetry. Without it the genre becomes paranormal romance.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Register — folk-horror-bestial (Eggers), gothic-decadent (Interview), art-melancholy (Only Lovers Left Alive), high-school-romantic (Twilight), Southern-pulp (True Blood), action-vampire (Blade, Underworld), mockumentary-comic (WWDITS). The recipe runs across all.
- Mortal age and posture — child (Let the Right One In), teen (Twilight, Lost Boys), young adult (Buffy, Vampire Diaries), middle-aged (Only Lovers, AMC's Interview's Daniel), elderly (rarely; underused).
- Sunlight rule — kills (classical), burns (Underworld), sparkles (Twilight), weakens (True Blood), nothing (some daywalkers). Pick deliberately; the audience reads it as register marker.
- Coven structure — sibling (Interview's Lestat / Louis / Claudia), court (Underworld), family (Cullens), gang (Lost Boys), bureaucracy (True Blood's American Vampire League), house-share (WWDITS). The shape carries thematic weight.
- The Mortal's specific quality — blood line (Buffy's slayers, Twilight's singer scent), grief (Eggers' Ellen), intellect (Discovery of Witches), loneliness (Let the Right One In). What about THIS Mortal lands different.
- Era and setting — historical (Dracula, Nosferatu), small-town (Twilight, Let the Right One In, True Blood), urban (Interview, Blade, Only Lovers), period-roaming (Interview). Era is a register dial.
- Ending — converted, refused, destroyed-by-Hunter, escaped-into-eternity, mutually-destroyed. All five live in the corpus; pick for the cocktail.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the Mortal's invitations establish consent; the Sire and Coven turn desire into property; the Anchor makes damnation cost a specific life) — every token, rite, pursuit, and final verdict varies freely.

## Caveats
- Failure mode: the vampire as monster-of-the-week. If the predator has no interiority, the seduction has nowhere to land. Long memory is non-negotiable.
- Failure mode: the romance without the horror. Twilight is criticized for sanding the predation; the recipe holds only if the cost shows somewhere — even sparkles must occasionally bare fangs.
- Failure mode: the horror without the romance. Eggers' Nosferatu still stages Orlok's pull on Ellen as desire. If the audience never wants the vampire to win, the cocktail has collapsed to monster horror.
- Failure mode: the Mortal as object. The Mortal must be a hungry POV — bored, grieving, seeking. The Vampire is the offer; the Mortal must reach.
- Failure mode: turning treated as upgrade. Conversion must be death-and-sequel — something is lost. If turning is just power-up, the damnation half of the cocktail evaporates.
- Register dial: comedy (WWDITS) works because it preserves the recipe scrupulously — the housemates ARE a coven, the familiar IS exploited, the bite IS sex — and lets the audience laugh at how seriously the conventions are held.
- Genre proximity: paranormal romance softens the damnation; monster horror discards the seduction. Vampire-as-recipe lives in the middle where both engines fire. Drift either direction and you have left the slot.
