# Innocent + Impossible Tech Companion vs Hostile System

## Slot
Animated serialized sci-fi where an INNOCENT (often a child or young adult) finds, recovers, or wakes an IMPOSSIBLE TECH COMPANION (creature, robot, AI, hybrid) that the surrounding SYSTEM views as forbidden, dangerous, or obsolete — and must protect it through to a saving act that uses the protagonist's specific non-combat competence. Stray Signal lives here.

(Tonal range: Wall-E's silent-warm through Wild Robot's family-naturalistic to Edgerunners' brutal-tragic. Same recipe, opposite registers.)

## Emotional contract
**Wonder + protection + ache — driven by recognition.** The reader/viewer falls in love with the Companion in the pilot, then carries dread the System will take it away, then watches the Innocent's specific competence save it (and pays in something — the Mentor, a town, the Innocent's old life). The ache is what they take home. The genre's addictive beat is **recognition**: "everyone else saw a thing; I saw a someone." The story does not merely keep the Companion alive — it forces the world to **reclassify** it, from tool / weapon / monster to person.

## Trope vocabulary

**Genre-canonical tropes (deployable):**
- **The Pivot Beat** — In the pilot, the Companion's first appearance is dangerous (Mule attacks, Iron Giant looms, dragon almost kills), then turns into companionship within the same episode. Violence into care. The tonal whiplash of the pilot.
- **The Threshold Object** — A small physical thing that anchors the relationship — trinks, an egg, a spark plug, a found cassette, a glowing seed. Toyetic and merchandisable. The marketing.
- **The Tired Bureaucrat** — Uncle / parent figure who works for the System but isn't a villain. Their tiredness is the audience's permission to see the System as system, not as evil.
- **The Found Family** — A small group of Innocent + Friend + maybe one Mentor forms; not blood-related; bonded by the Companion's secret.
- **The First Detection** — System's instruments notice the Companion's signature (power draw, anomalous frequency, missing inventory item). Alarm cascade begins offstage.
- **The Saving Competence** — The Innocent's specific non-combat skill (Six sorting trinks by feel; Hiccup understanding dragon body language; Hogarth drawing) is what saves the Companion at the climax. NOT combat. NOT a chosen-one power.
- **The Mentor's Sacrifice** — The Mentor / Memory character is wounded, lost, or captured to mark the stakes. Their final act is to give the Innocent the missing piece of world-history that explains the Companion.
- **The Wonder Beat (per episode)** — Every episode has one moment where the Innocent and Companion share something private — touch, breath, light, sound, a small game. This is the show's emotional currency.
- **The Hidden Layer** — The world has hidden layers (Old Town below the Seam, the depths below the surface, the wilderness beyond the rangers). The Companion comes from down/back/beyond.
- **The Proof of Soul** — At the climax the Companion does something its function cannot account for: it sacrifices itself, refuses an order, protects instead of performs. A tool completes a task; a soul chooses a meaning. (Per the anti-trope, the Companion survives the sacrifice — dormant, scattered, rebooted — but the choice is real and irreversible.)
- **The Quiet Defeat of the System** — At the end, the System is not destroyed — it is OUTLASTED. Wall-E's robots ignore directives. Iron Giant's gun is gone. Hiccup convinces the village. The system goes on being a system, but the Companion stays.

**Anti-tropes (the genre does NOT do these):**
- Don't make the Companion a weapon — even if it CAN be (Iron Giant could destroy a city; he chooses not to). The Companion's potential violence is restrained by its bond to the Innocent.
- Don't let the Innocent's saving competence be combat ability. They never win by fighting harder. They win by SEEING what others miss.
- Don't make the System's Face a stupid villain — they must be competent, intelligent, and from a coherent worldview. The audience must understand why they think they're right.
- Don't permanently kill the Companion. It can be lost, dormant, scattered — but the door stays open. (Wall-E reboots. Iron Giant reassembles. Wild Robot returns.)
- Don't reveal the world's true history in dialogue — the Mentor SHOWS it (an old photograph, a scar, a working machine in the basement). The reader earns the worldbuilding through observation.
- Don't have the Tired Adult start as the villain. They must read as background until they tip.

## Conformity rules
- The Companion must be VISUALLY DISTINCTIVE and TOYETIC. A reader/viewer should be able to draw it from memory after one episode.
- Episode 1 must end on a wonder beat — the Innocent and Companion sharing a quiet moment that establishes the relationship as the show's emotional currency.
- The Tired Adult must be SYMPATHETIC, not malevolent. Their tiredness is the show's nuance — the System has them too.
- The climax must turn on the Innocent's NON-COMBAT competence — a skill demonstrated in Episodes 1-3, at full extension. No power-leveling, no suddenly-discovered combat ability; they win by SEEING what the System refuses to see. It pairs with the Companion's Proof of Soul (a sacrifice or a refusal of its function) — one or both carries the climax, but the Innocent never wins by fighting harder.
- The Companion must not be a weapon — even if it CAN be. Its restraint IS the audience's catharsis.
- Multi-generational appeal: the show must work for the child watching the surface (visual delight + character bond) AND the older viewer reading the references (worldbuilding + thematic argument).
- The System must persist at the end. The show is not "defeating evil" — it is "preserving wonder against erasure" AND forcing a reclassification: by the finale the world, or a meaningful part of it, must treat the Companion as a someone rather than a something. The System survives; its category error does not.
- The Innocent's wound and the Companion's wound must MIRROR — both abandoned, obsolete, misclassified, or treated as a tool. The bond is earned because each recognizes its own condition in the other; it is not just cute proximity.

## Wiring tests
- Delete-test: no episode can be removed without a later episode breaking.
- The climax consumes at least two character choices from distinct earlier episodes.
- Every named flaw fires as a choice whose consequence is visible in a later episode.
- Every relationship the cast table says moves shows its early state AND its changed
  state as on-screen actions.
- Every cost charged is paid: a later episode is harder in a named way because of it.

## Variation rules
- Setting — rural (Iron Giant), urban (Wall-E), vertical city (Stray Signal), wilderness (Wild Robot), space (Treasure Planet), abyss (Made in Abyss).
- Companion type — organic (dragon), mechanical (giant, Wall-E), digital (Pantheon's uploads), hybrid (Big Hero 6's Baymax).
- Tonal register — warm (Iron Giant, Wild Robot), bittersweet (Wall-E, ET), kinetic (Big Hero 6), brutal (Edgerunners), eerie (Made in Abyss).
- Friend type — peer (Six's Duds), oddball (Wild Robot's geese), mentor-Friend hybrid (Hogarth's beatnik).
- The System's specific aesthetic — bureaucratic-grey (Corporation), militarized (Iron Giant), corporate-cheerful (Wall-E), feudal (Treasure Planet).
- Whether the Companion speaks — most don't (Wall-E mostly silent; Iron Giant minimal; dragon body language). The voicing is a register dial.
- Whether the Mentor is also the Maker — a separate eccentric who only remembers (Hogarth's beatnik), or the Companion's creator / the Innocent's lost parent (Tenma in Astro Boy, Tadashi in Big Hero 6, Silver-as-surrogate-father in Treasure Planet). Fused, the season carries an inheritance argument — "did we make a child or a tool"; separate, Episode 7 stays a pure world-history reveal.
- The wiring's edges are fixed (the discovery becomes a trace, the hunt burns safety, the revealed history enables public proof) while the content of every edge varies freely: which competence, what object, whose sacrifice, what form the reclassification takes.

## Caveats
- Project-specific recipe — derived to serve Stray Signal, listed in the methodology library as project-internal rather than a generalized slot.
- The recipe sits ABOVE the divergence pipeline's persona conditioning. If a persona's polemic conflicts with the recipe's conformity rules, the recipe wins. Persona becomes a register dial above the floor.
- Common failure mode: the System's Face becoming process-functional (inspector / cleaner) instead of human-motivated. The antagonist must carry a personal wound or humiliation the anomaly threatens to expose.
- Common failure mode (observed in stray runs 001-005): the persona pulls toward adult-prestige register, the Companion gets abstracted into a McGuffin (timer / signal / glitch), and the Innocent's competence becomes generic agency. The recipe's job is to lock the Companion-as-living-thing and the competence-as-specific-skill commitments back in.
- Tonal register is a free dial — Edgerunners and Iron Giant are the same recipe.
