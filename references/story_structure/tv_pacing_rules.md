# TV Pacing Rules

## Episode Structures

### 30-Minute (22 minutes actual)

**Standard Structure**:
- Cold Open: 1-2 minutes
- Act 1: 5-6 minutes
- Act 2: 6-7 minutes
- Act 3: 6-7 minutes
- Tag: 1-2 minutes

**Beat Density**: Higher—beats happen faster, less breathing room.

### 45-Minute (42 minutes actual)

**Standard Structure**:
- Cold Open: 2-3 minutes
- Act 1: 8-10 minutes
- Act 2: 10-12 minutes
- Act 3: 10-12 minutes
- Act 4: 8-10 minutes
- Tag: 1-2 minutes

**Beat Density**: Moderate—room for both action and character.

### 60-Minute (52-58 minutes actual)

**Standard Structure**:
- Cold Open: 3-5 minutes
- Act 1: 8-12 minutes
- Act 2: 10-12 minutes
- Act 3: 10-12 minutes
- Act 4: 10-12 minutes
- Act 5: 8-10 minutes
- Tag: 1-2 minutes

**Beat Density**: Lower—room for complexity and subplots.

## Act-Out Requirements

Each act must end with a reason to return:

### Act 1: The Question
- Establish the central problem
- End on: "What will they do about it?"
- Strength: Medium

### Act 2: The Complication
- First approach fails or reveals new information
- End on: "How can they overcome this?"
- Strength: Medium-High

### Act 3: The Midpoint Turn
- Major revelation or shift in understanding
- End on: "Everything has changed"
- Strength: High

### Act 4: The Dark Moment
- Lowest point, all seems lost
- End on: "Is there any hope?"
- Strength: Highest

### Act 5: The Resolution
- Climax and aftermath
- End on: Character growth or new status quo
- Strength: Emotional satisfaction

## Pacing Principles

### Scene Length Variety

- Never three same-length scenes in a row
- Alternate between short punchy scenes and longer developed scenes
- Action scenes: Shorter
- Dialogue scenes: Medium
- Emotional scenes: Can be longer

### Energy Modulation

```
High ████████░░░░████████░░████████████
Low  ░░░░░░░░████░░░░░░░░██░░░░░░░░░░░░
     Cold  Act1   Act2   Act3   Act4   Act5
```

- Don't sustain high energy for too long
- Quiet moments make loud moments louder
- Comic relief provides energy valleys

### Story Thread Weaving

**A-Story** (Main Plot): 60-70%
- Gets majority of screen time
- Drives episode forward
- Contains main conflict

**B-Story** (Relationship/Character): 25-30%
- Complements A-story thematically
- Provides contrast
- Often more emotional

**C-Story** (Runner): 5-10%
- Comic relief or world-building
- Very light touch
- Optional in some episodes

### Thread Distribution by Act

| Act | A-Story | B-Story | C-Story |
|-----|---------|---------|---------|
| 1 | Setup | Introduce | Plant |
| 2 | Develop | Develop | Touch |
| 3 | Complicate | Crisis | Touch |
| 4 | Dark moment | Connection | Payoff |
| 5 | Resolve | Resolve | - |

## Genre-Specific Pacing

### Procedural (Case-of-the-Week)

- A-Story: The case
- B-Story: Character/relationship
- Case drives structure, character provides depth
- Pilot needs more character time than typical episode

### Serialized Drama

- A-Story: Season arc movement
- B-Story: Episode-specific conflict
- More connective tissue between episodes
- Cliffhangers more common

### Dramedy

- Faster dialogue pace
- More frequent scene changes
- Comedy beats required per act
- Emotional moments earn more weight

### Thriller/Mystery

- Information reveal pacing critical
- Red herrings need setup and payoff
- Tension builds across acts
- Climax is revelation + confrontation

## Scene Pacing

### Dialogue Scenes

- Enter late, leave early
- Cut on the interesting line
- Don't let characters finish conversations politely
- Subtext > Text

### Action Scenes

- Clear geography before chaos
- Punctuate with character beats
- Build to peak, then quick resolution
- Consequences matter more than spectacle

### Emotional Scenes

- Earn the moment with setup
- Give space for performance
- Don't rush the feeling
- Physical action can carry emotion

## Commercial Break Considerations

For traditional TV:

- Each act out should work as mini-cliffhanger
- Viewer should want to return after break
- Avoid resolving tension right before break
- First scene after break: Quick reorientation

For streaming:

- Act structure still valuable for pacing
- Can use more subtle act transitions
- Cliffhangers drive "next episode" instead of "after break"
- Episode endings especially important

## Common Pacing Problems

### "Act 2 Sag"
- Middle feels slow
- Solution: Add complication, raise stakes, introduce new element

### "Rush to Resolution"
- Ending feels abrupt
- Solution: Add beats in final act, let moments breathe

### "Slow Start"
- Takes too long to engage
- Solution: Stronger cold open, faster inciting incident

### "Subplot Overwhelm"
- Too many threads, none satisfying
- Solution: Reduce subplots, focus resources

### "Exposition Dumps"
- Information stopping story
- Solution: Deliver info through conflict, or cut

## Notes

- Pacing is feel more than formula
- Read scripts aloud to test flow
- Beats per page: 2-3 for dialogue, more for action
- Trust the structure, then break it intentionally
